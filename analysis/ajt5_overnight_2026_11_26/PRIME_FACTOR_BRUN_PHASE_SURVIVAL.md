# A third-order CRT sieve for arbitrary phases past union budget one

The [true-origin phase model](STRATIFIED_ARBITRARY_PHASE_SURVIVAL.md) permits external inert primes at arbitrary legal phases while the remaining primes are set to zero phase. Put \(z=\sqrt{\log X}\). There is a family of eligible large primes in \((z/2,z]\) for which the grouped one-axis blocking budget exceeds 1, yet **every phase assignment** leaves \(\gg z^3\) positive-index surviving cells inside the real activity box. This uses a third-order truncated CRT sieve, rather than estimating intersections of ungrouped primes.

## Grouped one-axis bound

Freeze a row; let the changing exponent index be \(Bt\). For each nonzero-phase prime q take a prime divisor \(\ell\) of the order of the stepped base in that axis. A weak hit modulo q forces t into at most one residue class modulo that full order, hence into at most one modulo \(\ell\). Group all constraints sharing \(\ell\) into their union of forbidden residues \(F_\ell\), with \(r_\ell=|F_\ell|\), \(a_\ell=r_\ell/\ell\), and \(\mu=\sum a_\ell\). Using exact CRT between **distinct** factors, one through three groups have \(\prod r_\ell\) allowed intersections of forbidden residues and a counting error less than \(\prod r_\ell\) in N consecutive integers. Bonferroni then bounds the number escaping even these enlarged constraints below by
\[
 N(1-\mu+e_2(a)-e_3(a))-\sum_{k=1}^3 e_k(r),
\]
where \(e_k\) is the elementary symmetric sum. Weak avoidance also implies avoidance of the exact-valuation-one certificates.

## Existence of a growing one-axis family

Consider primes \(\ell\in(z^{23/50},z^{12/25}]\). For \(q\in(z/2,z]\), \(q\equiv3\bmod4,\ q\equiv1\bmod\ell\), the [Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/mathematika/article/abs/on-the-large-sieve/4AE92826645F6F3393D30AF0D2767346) gives aggregate absolute count error \(O_A(z/\log^A z)\) across these moduli, around \((\operatorname{li}(z)-\operatorname{li}(z/2))/(2(\ell-1))\) per \(\ell\). If both base orders omit \(\ell\), the subgroup generated by 9 and 25 has order at most \(Y=z^{27/50}\). The count of such q (irrespective of \(\ell\)) is \(O(Y^{3/2}/\log z)\): among \((a,b)\) in a square of side \(\lceil\sqrt Y\rceil+1\) there is a collision \(9^a25^b\). Then q divides one of \(O(Y)\) nonzero integers \(9^{u_+}25^{v_+}-9^{u_-}25^{v_-}\), with \(|u|,|v|\le \lceil\sqrt Y\rceil\) and log size \(O(\sqrt Y)\). They are nonzero by multiplicative independence. Since each q has at most two \(\ell\) in the interval dividing \(q-1\), deleting all these q loses total multiplicity \(O(Y^{3/2}/\log z)\).

Discard also the finite panel. The groups with fewer than 256 remaining q have reciprocal weight \(o(1)\): for each such group the sum of its absolute count error and deletion count is \(\gg z/(\ell\log z)\); summing these inequalities gives the assertion. The remaining reciprocal weight tends to \(\log(24/23)\) by the prime number theorem and partial summation. Each remaining group has at least 128 q whose order contains \(\ell\) in one of the axes, assigning that group to an axis with at least this many. The better axis has reciprocal weight at least \(\tfrac12\log(24/23)-o(1)\). Select up to 64 q in each chosen group, all distinct across groups. This is possible by Hall's theorem, applied to 64 copies of each group: every group has at least 128 neighbors and each q belongs to at most two groups, so any collection of groups has at least 64 times as many neighbors. Since \(32\log(24/23)>6/5\), select a subcollection (including partial groups) whose weight \(\sum 1/\ell\) counting each assigned q lies in \([7/6,6/5]\); increments are \(o(1)\). The number of chosen primes is \(\gg z^{23/50}\). None of these \(q-1\) can also have a prime factor above \(z^{11/20}\), because the product would exceed z, so they are distinct from the type used in the earlier large-factor result.

## Applying the sieve in the actual box

Allow arbitrary legal phases at these chosen primes and all tiny primes \(q\le(\log z)/3\); set the other phases to zero. Protect both axes at the tiny primes by taking steps divisible by the corresponding orders modulo \(q^2\) (each step \(\le z^{1/3+o(1)}\) as in the earlier note). This cannot remove the newly chosen medium prime factors of the large-prime orders. Use a grid of \(N=\lfloor z^{3/2}\rfloor\) values per stepped axis, lying in the real \(O(z^2)\) box. At each frozen row the actual \(r_\ell\le64\), so \(\mu\le6/5\) irrespective of phase. Because \(\max a_\ell=o(1)\), \(e_2(a)=\mu^2/2+o(1)\) and \(e_3(a)=\mu^3/6+o(1)\) uniformly. The cubic \(1-\mu+\mu^2/2-\mu^3/6\) decreases on this range and is at least \(29/125\). The error sum is \(O(z^{36/25})=o(N)\), since the number of groups is \(O(z^{12/25})\). Therefore each of N rows has at least \((29/125-o(1))N\) survivors. The coordinate CRT realizes all such legal phases with true origin in the height band as before. This does not establish survival under arbitrary active phases at every external prime or a true norm at any other surviving cell.

As a small independent check on the finite sieve formula, take distinct moduli 5, 7, 11 and forbidden sets of sizes 2, 3, 4. Direct enumeration over their full period gives the same cubic inclusion-exclusion main term and survivor count, since there are only three events.

~~~javascript
const q=[5,7,11], r=[2,3,4], n=5*7*11;
let survivors=0;
for(let x=0;x<n;x++) if(q.every((p,i)=>x%p>=r[i])) survivors++;
const e1=r[0]*q[1]*q[2]+q[0]*r[1]*q[2]+q[0]*q[1]*r[2];
const e2=r[0]*r[1]*q[2]+r[0]*q[1]*r[2]+q[0]*r[1]*r[2];
if(e1<=n||survivors!==n-e1+e2-r[0]*r[1]*r[2]||survivors!==84)
  throw Error("Brun replay");
~~~
