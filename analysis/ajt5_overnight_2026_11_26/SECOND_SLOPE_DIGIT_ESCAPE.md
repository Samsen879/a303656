# Changing the second primitive-slope digit on the same target

Let c=c_0+3Mx and d=d_0+3My with 3 not dividing M c_0 d_0. If lambda_*=d_0/c_0 mod 9 and lambda_0=lambda_* mod 3, the slope lambda_*+3t mod 9 is exactly M(y-lambda_0 x)=t c_0 mod 3. Changing only the multiplier of a ray through the same anchor does not move this next slope digit; its intercept must change. For a nonzero reader with depth-one step characters X=3^(3M), Y=5^(3M), set A=3^c_0, B=5^d_0 modulo that reader. If Y has order three, a hit at x requires U=(n-A X^x)/B in the subgroup generated by Y, and occurs at the unique offset t=M c_0^(-1)(log_Y U-lambda_0 x) mod 3. If Y=1, the hit is independent of the offset.

Three such nonzero readers can cover the whole available three-by-three plane only if all three are single-axis readers covering distinct parallel stripes. A single-axis reader hits at most three cells. In a two-axis reader, both X,Y generate the cube roots of unity. A collision between equal nonzero values at different cells requires both coordinate multipliers to change. They cannot be equal; opposite multipliers force the two summands' ratio to be a cube root. Thus two equal values are possible only for coinciding cosets. In that case diagonal sums lie in 2 times the cube roots and off-diagonal sums in minus the cube roots, with at most two hits at a nonzero value. Those two sets are disjoint for these primes greater than 5. For inert ternary-active primes, odd c and even d make the summands' ratio a nonsquare, so there is at most one hit. A two-axis reader and two stripes therefore cover at most eight cells (seven with those parity conditions). This statement requires that the rest of the panel permit the plane under consideration.

At the exact target from NESTED_TERNARY_READER_ESCAPE.md, all nine cells are positive and pass the four zero readers. At (c,d)=(1,2392), slope 7 mod 9, the remainder also passes all five retained nonzero readers, breaking the old fixed-slope cover. Its exact valuation one at the omitted prime 43 means it is not a sum of two squares. No general height or density change is claimed.

```python
from itertools import product
from math import prod

Z = (1783, 5023, 11419697846380955982026777206637491, 163)
P = (19, 127, 199, 271, 4159)
S = 2310
f = [3**(1+S*j) + 5**(82+S*j) for j in range(9)]
pairs = [(9,24)] + [(0,q) for q in Z]
pairs += [(f[j]+p,p*p) for p,j in zip(P,(0,1,2,2,5))]
M = prod(m for _,m in pairs)
u = sum(a*(M//m)*pow(M//m,-1,m) for a,m in pairs) % M
n = f[-1]+1+(u-f[-1]-1) % M

assert n.bit_length() == 43100
assert all((n-f[j]) % (p*p) == p for j,p in enumerate(P[:3]))
assert [(pow(3,S,p),pow(5,S,p)) for p in P[:3]] == [
    (7,7), (107,1), (92,1)]

R = {}
for x,y in product(range(3), repeat=2):
    c,d = 1+S*x,82+S*y
    assert c%6 == 1 and d%6 == 4
    assert d*pow(c,-1,9)%9 == 1+3*(2*(y-x)%3)
    R[x,y] = n-3**c-5**d
    assert R[x,y] > 0 and all(R[x,y]%q for q in Z)

hits = [{cell for cell,r in R.items() if r%p == 0} for p in P]
assert hits == [{(0,0)}, {(1,y) for y in range(3)},
                {(2,y) for y in range(3)}, {(x,2) for x in range(3)}, set()]
assert R[0,2] % 271**2 == 221*271
assert all(R[0,1]%p for p in Z+P)
assert R[0,1] % 43**2 == 34*43
print("Same target, full-panel escape at (1,2392), exact v_43 = 1")

# Independently check the two-axis cardinality lemma for small ternary fields.
for p in (7,13,19,31,37,43):
    mu=[j for j in range(1,p) if j**3%p==1]
    assert len(mu)==3
    for a in range(1,p):
        for b in range(1,p):
            values=[(a*u+b*v)%p for u,v in product(mu, repeat=2)]
            assert all(values.count(t)<=2 for t in set(values)-{0})
            if pow(a*pow(b,-1,p)%p,(p-1)//2,p)==p-1:
                assert all(values.count(t)<=1 for t in set(values)-{0})
print("Independent finite checks of two-axis collision cardinalities")
```
