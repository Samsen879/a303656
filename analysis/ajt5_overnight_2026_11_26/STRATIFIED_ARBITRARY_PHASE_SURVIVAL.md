# Short surviving cells under growing families of arbitrary phases

Keep the fixed panel and true origin \(n=2+u^2+v^2\) from [the proportional-box cover](PROPORTIONAL_BOX_EXTERNAL_COVER.md). Let \(z=\sqrt{\log X}\) and let Q be the usual external inert prime window up to z. Allow arbitrary legal phases at all \(q\in Q\) up to \((\log z)/3\), and at a chosen set of at most \(c z^{11/15}\) primes in \((z/2,z]\) whose orders of both 9 and 25 contain a common prime factor \(\ell_q>z^{11/20}\). Set all other external phases zero modulo q for n. For sufficiently small absolute c and sufficiently large X, **every such assignment** leaves \(\gg z^2\) non-origin even-even cells with both indices positive and \(i,j\le z^{4/3+o(1)}\) surviving exact-valuation-one certificates. These indices fit the actual active rectangle in every height band \([X,2X)\). The [axial-period result](AXIAL_PERIOD_AND_MIXED_RETURN.md) supplies \(\gg z/\log z\) eligible large primes; the claim does not permit them all to be arbitrary simultaneously.

## Proof

If \(n\equiv0\bmod q\), two nonzero squares cannot sum to n modulo an inert q, so the cell automatically survives there. At a small prime with \(n\not\equiv0,2\bmod q\), it suffices to return both powers to 1 modulo q. If \(n\equiv2\bmod q\), it suffices to return both modulo \(q^2\), because the true origin makes \(n-2\equiv0\bmod q^2\) then. Take the least common multiples of the respective small-prime orders as steps \(B_9,B_{25}\). Each divides \(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,\lfloor(\log z)/3\rfloor)\): each order divides \(q(q-1)\) with coprime factors at most that cutoff. Thus each step is at most \(z^{1/3+o(1)}\) by the prime number theorem.

Search the subgrid \(i=B_9 k,\ j=B_{25}r\) for \(1\le k,r\le\lceil z\rceil\). The large factor \(\ell_q\) survives in both new orders, since it exceeds all possible prime factors of the steps. Let \(\Gamma,\Pi\) be the two multiplicative subgroups generated by the stepped bases modulo q, and \(T=|\Gamma||\Pi|>z^{11/10}\). A certificate requires the two powered terms to sum to n modulo q. For zero n-residue there are no solutions. For a nonzero target and \(T<q^{3/2}\), [the Mit'kin subgroup intersection bound, Lemma 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1302.3839#page=10) with a single coset pair bounds the number of solutions on complete periods by \(O(T^{1/3})\); the relative count is \(O(T^{-2/3})\). When \(T\ge q^{3/2}\), expand the two subgroup indicators into multiplicative characters and sum over pairs with the specified nonzero sum. The trivial-trivial term contributes at most \(T/q\) and the rest at most \(\sqrt q\) altogether: each normalized Jacobi sum term is at most \(\sqrt q\) before averaging over the characters. This gives relative count \(O(1/q+\sqrt q/T)\). Both cases imply \(O(z^{-11/15})\), uniformly in the phase. Since the two periods are at most \(q-1\le z\), every residue occurs in the truncated grid at most twice its average multiplicity in either axis. The total fraction covered by any of the chosen large primes is therefore \(O(c)\) by the union bound, leaving at least half for small absolute c.

Any legal choices for the phases can be realized together by coordinate CRT and the original height construction: at each prime lift a pair representing the specified norm residue modulo \(q^2\) (legality already supplies such a pair), with \(n\equiv0\bmod q^2\) permitted at zero-phase primes. The combined modulus has logarithm \(O(z\log z)=o(\log X)\). Window survival here only concerns the specified valuation-one certificates; it is not a true two-square assertion beyond the origin.

## Exact finite uniform control without subgroup estimates

In a \(K\times N\) box with two periods \(R',S'\) modulo q, fixed rows (or columns) have at most one inspected residue class on the other axis: the target powered value is either absent or unique within its period. This bounds the number of hits at any phase by \(\min(N\lceil K/R'\rceil,K\lceil N/S'\rceil)\). For \(323\times221\) and primes \(7,11,19,23\) the union bound guarantees at least 18,564 survivors even when all four phases are arbitrary.

~~~javascript
function order(b,q) {
  let x=b%q, n=1;
  while (x!==1) { x=x*b%q; n++; }
  return n;
}
const K=323, N=221;
let covered=0;
for (const q of [7,11,19,23]) {
  const R=order(9,q), S=order(25,q);
  covered += Math.min(N*Math.ceil(K/R),K*Math.ceil(N/S));
}
if (K*N-covered!==18564) throw Error("incorrect finite bound");
~~~
