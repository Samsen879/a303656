# Positive-proportion even norm matching with a Gaussian gcd signature

In the same \(c,d\) box as [the original two-axis result](POSITIVE_PROPORTION_TWO_AXIS_NORM_PAIRS.md), a positive proportion of \(n\in[X,2X]\) have \(\gg_\epsilon(\log X)^{2-2\log 2-\epsilon}\) marked two-square pairs with rows disjoint on both exponent axes. Here take \(n\equiv2515388059284\bmod4236443047200\). The rational gcd of the two residuals in each selected row is \(10\). From the left residuals of three distinct rows one can choose Gaussian certificates with consecutive Gaussian gcd norms \(2,10\). This is an unsigned result, scoped to the displayed progression. The original odd progression cannot have a Gaussian gcd divisible by \(1+i\), because every residual is odd.

For a row put \(\Delta=3^c(3^{24}-1)\), \(R_+=n-3^c-5^d\), \(R_-=R_+-\Delta\). The new progression is the CRT prescription \(n=20\bmod32,\ n=9\bmod25,\ n=3^{26}\bmod p\) for \(p=3,7,13,41,73,6481\). The factorization \(3^{24}-1=2^5\cdot 5\cdot7\cdot13\cdot41\cdot73\cdot6481\) shows that these are all places relevant to the rational gcd. Both residuals are \(18\bmod32\), their residues modulo \(25\) are \(5,10\), and they are units at the other primes dividing \(\Delta\). Hence the rational gcd is \(10\).

Repeat [the primitive line construction](FIXED_SHIFT_TRUE_NORM_PAIRS.md). Write \(z_\pm=w\pm a\), \(a\cdot w=\Delta/4\). Modulo \(32\) choose \(a=(1,0),w=(\Delta/4,1)\); modulo \(25\), and modulo \(29^2,37^2\) to retain the exact markers, split the norm into two linear factors. At 5, both prescribed norms have valuation one. Choose opposite isotropic lines for the two vectors modulo 5, and lift on each line using a unit split coordinate; this ensures they differ modulo 5. At 29 and 37 the norm residues differ modulo the prime because the prime does not divide \(\Delta\). At other fixed primes dividing \(\Delta\), choose two opposite representing vectors for the common nonzero norm. Halving their sum and difference gives \(w,a\) mod these odd moduli, with \(a\) nonzero at each prime. CRT thus gives a fixed modulus and a nonempty local class; 29 and 37 refine the row, not the common displayed progression.

We may further require **no inert prime divisor of \(R_+\)** without losing the \(\gg X\) uniform row mass. For \(p\equiv3\bmod4\) below a fixed cutoff \(P\), outside the fixed CRT modulus, exclude on the primitive \(a\)-box the \(p+1\) vectors of norm \(-\Delta/4\ne0\) out of \(p^2-1\) nonzero vectors. Then \(a\cdot(w+a)\not\equiv0\bmod p\); anisotropy implies \(p\nmid R_+\). The CRT/Möbius count retains \(\gg 1/\log P\), by the product of \(1-1/(p-1)\) (even taking all sufficiently large primes gives this lower bound). This does not constrain \(w\) at the extra primes, so its line step stays fixed relative to \(\|a\|\), and the lower bound is \(\gg X/\log P\) with a lower constant independent of the fixed \(P\).

For larger inert \(p\) dividing \(R_+\), necessarily \(p^2\mid R_+\) and \(r_2(R_+)=r_2(R_+/p^2)\). For \(P<p\le X^{1/8}\), use the same [corrected Henriot two-form estimate](DEDUPLICATED_TRUE_NORM_SHIFT_COUNT.md) on \(m\) and \(p^2m-\Delta\), over an interval of length \(\asymp X/p^2\ge X^{3/4}\). The coefficients have size \(O(X^{1/4})\); the forms are primitive as \(p\nmid\Delta\). At good primes the two-form first-moment factors cancel as before; the exceptional prime support adds only \(p\). Thus the upper mass for this \(p\) is \(O(X/p^2)\) uniformly. For \(p>X^{1/8}\) up to \(O(\sqrt X)\), elementary multiple counts give \(O(X^{7/8}+\sqrt X)\), times a subpower bound for representation multiplicities. Total removal is \(O(X/P)+o(X)\). Taking \(P\) sufficiently large and fixed leaves \(\gg X\) mass. The modular restrictions on the primitive box only concern that fixed \(P\); taking \(X\) large after \(P\) is sufficient.

All remaining second-moment and weighted bipartite matching bounds from the original result apply unchanged, since every mask only decreases a nonnegative \(r_2\) product. For three left norms from distinct matched rows, \(v_2=v_5=1\) and all other prime factors split in the Gaussian integers. At every split prime other than 5, put the whole prime power of the first and third certificates above one Gaussian prime, and that of the middle certificate above its conjugate. At 5 choose opposite directions for the first and second, and equal directions for the second and third. Gaussian unique factorization makes these choices simultaneous. Only \(1+i\) is shared by the first pair, and only \(1+i\) and one prime over 5 by the second; the gcd norms are \(2,10\).

As a separate finite check, outside the asymptotic exponent box, \(n=15224717200884\) belongs to the new progression and the labels \((10,18),(18,16),(6,4)\) have square coordinates \((299267,3364589),(3463337,-1754149),(-1583909,3565943)\), respectively. Signs of coordinates need not be positive in the Gaussian certificates.
