# Positive proportion of genuine coprime norm pairs across both exponent axes

For every sufficiently large \(X\), a positive proportion (relative to \(X\)) of integers \(n\in[X,2X]\) have two coprime composite two-square residuals at \((c,d),(c+24,d)\), in the original class \(n\equiv319055\bmod480480\), with exact markers \(29,37\). On a set of \(\gg_\epsilon X\) such targets one can simultaneously select \(\gg_\epsilon(\log X)^{2-2\log2-\epsilon}\) paired rows with pairwise different \(c\)'s and pairwise different \(d\)'s, for fixed \(0<\epsilon<2-2\log2\). This is an unweighted theorem in one fixed residue class, not a signed moment or pointwise coverage result.

## Uniform row count and moments

Write \(L=\log X\). Take the two step-120 exponent strings \(c\equiv26,\ d\equiv50\bmod120\) in the ranges \(X^{1/8}\le3^c,\ 3^{c+24}\le X^{1/4}\) and \(X^{1/3}\le5^d\le X^{1/2}\). Both contain \(\asymp L\) entries. At row \(i=(c,d)\) set \(s_i=3^c+5^d,\ t_i=3^{c+24}+5^d\). Let \(W_i(n)\) be the product of the two \(r_2\) counts, masked by the original progression, residual height window, coprimality and exact markers. Then \(\sum_n W_i(n)\gg X\) uniformly in the row. Indeed, the [primitive-vector CRT and line construction](FIXED_SHIFT_TRUE_NORM_PAIRS.md) uses only the prime support of \(3^c(3^{24}-1)\), which is fixed, and the exponents ensure the same required unit/parity conditions. Its CRT modulus can be fixed. Finitely many permitted local classes give a common lower constant in the primitive-box count, and the parallel displacement on the line is uniformly \(O(X^{-1/4})\) since the shift is \(O(X^{1/4})\). The prescribed norm interval and shifts put all these \(n\) in \([X,2X]\) for large \(X\).

The [uniform corrected Henriot bounds and local factors](DEDUPLICATED_TRUE_NORM_SHIFT_COUNT.md) give \(\sum_n W_i(n)^\lambda\ll_\lambda XL^{2^\lambda-2}\) for fixed \(\lambda\ge1\). For distinct rows all four shifts are distinct: if the \(d\)'s differ, their \(5\)-power separation exceeds the full \(3\)-power box; if the \(d\)'s agree use injectivity and the disjoint \(c,c+24\bmod120\) phases. For \(i\ne j\) the four-form \(\lambda=1\) bound is \(O(X)\prod_{p\mid\Delta}(1+C/p)\), where \(\Delta\ne0\) is the product of all six differences. Its worst-case extra factor is \((\log L)^{O(1)}\).

## Average over the second \(5\)-exponent

The two within-row differences contribute only a fixed prime support. Fix the two \(c\)'s and the first \(d\); average over the different second exponent \(e\). For \(p\ne5\), each of the four cross-differences gives at most one progression-index class modulo \(\operatorname{ord}_p(5^{120})\), or no class. Conditional collision probability is therefore \(O(1/\operatorname{ord}_p(5^{120})+1/L)\). Since \(\log|\Delta|=O(L)\), primes above \(L^2\) contribute \(1+O(1/L)\). Small-order primes with \(\operatorname{ord}_p(5^{120})\le p^{1/4}\) have summable reciprocals: in a dyadic interval \([Y,2Y]\) their product divides \(\prod_{k\le(2Y)^{1/4}}((5^{120})^k-1)\), whose log is \(O(Y^{1/2})\). For other primes above a fixed \(P\), use the [Jensen weights](AVERAGED_SHIFT_DISCRIMINANT.md) proportional to \(1/(p\log p)\), choosing \(P\) so that the powered local factors cost at most \(p^{1/8}\). The expected excess is bounded by a constant times
\[
 \sum_{P<p\le L^2}p^{-7/8}\left(p^{-1/4}+L^{-1}\right)=O(1).
\]
No independence is used. Thus different-\(d\) pairs contribute \(O(XL^4)\) in total. The \(O(L^3)\) distinct equal-\(d\) pairs cost \(O(XL^3(\log L)^{O(1)})\); the diagonal costs \(O(XL^4)\) by \(\lambda=2\). Hence the summed second moment is \(O(XL^4)\), against first moment \(\gg XL^2\). Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(\gg X\) distinct targets.

## Matching without reusing either exponent

Truncate each row weight to zero above \(H=L^{2\log2+\epsilon}\). The lost first moment is \(o(X)\) per row by the \(\lambda>1\) bound and \(2^\lambda-2=(2\log2)(\lambda-1)+O((\lambda-1)^2)\). Write \(S(n)\) for retained weight and \(U(n)\) for the sum of squares of the incident retained weight at each \(c\) and each \(d\) vertex in the bipartite row graph for this \(n\). The retained first and second moments give \(S(n)\gg L^2\) at \(\gg X\) targets (discard the small-\(S\) points and apply Cauchy–Schwarz). Expansion of \(U\) costs \(O(XL^2H)\): the diagonal uses \(W_i^2 1_{W_i\le H}\le H W_i\), while the \(O(L^3)\) pairs sharing an exponent cost \(O(XL^3(\log L)^{O(1)})\). Markov leaves \(\gg_\epsilon X\) of the large-\(S\) points with \(U(n)=O_\epsilon(L^2H)\). Endpoints of a maximal matching of size \(m\) cover all edges, so \(S(n)^2\le2mU(n)\). This gives \(m\gg_\epsilon L^2/H\). Taking a consistent endpoint from each paired row yields a collection of representations distinct on both exponent axes; taking both endpoints preserves different \(c\)'s but repeats the \(d\) within each pair.
