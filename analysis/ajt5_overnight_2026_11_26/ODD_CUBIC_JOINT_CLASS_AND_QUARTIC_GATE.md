# Joint ideal classes and a quartic gate for odd cubic parameters

Continue [the repeated-prime analysis](ODD_CUBIC_REPEAT_PRIMES_CLASS_ORDER.md) for \(E=\mathbb Q(i,\sqrt B)\), \(F^-=\mathbb Q(\eta),\ \eta=i\sqrt B,\ B=9633851,\ m=4816930\), and \(u=2-im+\eta\). Write \(t=A/D^3,\ w=Dz,\ w^2(w-D)=Au\), and let \(n_0\) be the primitive part of \(N_{E/F^-}(w)\).

The imaginary quadratic class group of discriminant \(-B\) is \(\mathbb Z/858\oplus\mathbb Z/2\), using \(G=[(11,\eta-2)]\) and \(H=[(173,\eta)]\) respectively. The exact replay enumerates all 1716 primitive reduced forms of this fundamental discriminant and verifies that the forms of \(G^aH^b\) are distinct for \(0\le a<858,\ 0\le b<2\), and \(G^{858}=1\). H has order two because 173 is ramified. It verifies the following orientations:

| p | r, for \((p,\eta-r)\) | class coordinates |
|---:|---:|---|
| 3 | 1 | \((209,0)\) |
| 7 | 5 | \((354,1)\) |
| 5 | 3 | \((551,0)\) |
| 17 | 10 | \((281,0)\) |
| 368793917 | 150471209 | \((505,0)\) |
| 3434854150155653 | 852912836291150 | \((686,0)\) |
| 173 | 0 | \((0,1)\) |
| 233 | 0 | \((429,1)\) |
| 239 | 0 | \((429,0)\) |

The class of a conjectured primitive ideal must be zero, summing its oriented split exponents and the remaining ramified exponents. This allows exact cancellation between different repeated primes. For example \(\mathfrak p_{3,1}^{12}\) and \(\mathfrak p_{7,5}^{22}\) are separately nonprincipal but their product is principal, with witness
\[
 \gamma=(809576208169+891454925\eta)/2.
\]
Its norm and its two oriented divisibilities are verified below. In particular an obstruction found by treating the two primes separately need not survive as a class-group obstruction.

## A refinement when the primitive ideal is square

Suppose \((n_0)=I^2\). The two-torsion classes are generated by the ramified primes 173 and 239 from the table. Multiplying I by a product of these ramified ideals makes it principal, so a generator \(\nu\) satisfies \(\nu^2=\pm q n_0\) for a rational integer \(q\mid173\cdot239\). Then \(N_{E/F^-}(z/\nu)\) is rational. As in the previous phase argument, write \(z=(r+is)v\nu\) with \(r,s\) primitive integers and \(v\in\mathbb Q(\sqrt B)^\times\).

Let \(C=(r+is)\nu,\ H_1=C^2\bar u,\ U=\Im H_1,\ V=\Im(CH_1)\). In a viable case \(V\ne0\): if \(V=U=0\), C would be real, and the ratio of z to its real rescaling would be real, impossible by the equation. Eliminating v from \(t u=z^2(z-1)\) as before gives \(t=-|C|^4\Im(C)U^2/V^3\); \(|C|^4\) is rational since \(\nu\in F^-\). Thus \(\Im(C)U^2(V^\sigma)^3\) must be rational, where \(\sigma\) flips \(\sqrt B\).

Now take \(p\nmid 2B N(u)\) split in \(F^-\) but inert in \(E/F^-\), with a nonzero primitive-ideal exponent at one of its two branches. Work over \(\mathbb F_{p^2}\) with \(i=\iota,\ \iota^2=-1\), using the same iota for the two branch evaluations. On the selected branch \(\nu\) is zero and on the other it is nonzero. Since \(r,s\) are primitive, \(C_+=0,\ C_-=c\ne0\). If \(u_+=a\), conjugation exchanges the two branch values while extracting the imaginary component by division by \(2\iota\). Hence at the selected branch the values of \(\Im C,U,V\) are \(-c/(2\iota),-c^2a/(2\iota),-c^3a/(2\iota)\). On real coefficients \(\sigma\) is Frobenius in the residue algebra at this prime (p is 3 modulo 4 and B nonsquare). Comparing the above rational product with its Frobenius conjugate gives
\[
 (c/c^p)^4=a^p/a .
\]
Raising to \((p+1)/4\) implies \(a^{(p^2-1)/4}=1\). Thus **u must be a fourth power on the selected branch**, a stronger condition than the square test.

At 3 the only branch previously allowed by the square test is \(\eta\equiv1\), and there \(u=-\iota\) does not pass the fourth-power test. Therefore \(\pm3^k\) is impossible as a numerator for every \(k\ge0\): either the primitive norm is rational, already excluded, or it is supported only at 3 with even exponent, a square ideal violating the quartic gate. The same proof excludes \(\pm3^{12}7^{22}\): any nontrivial primitive ideal on these two allowed branches is constrained by principality, neither single-prime term at these exponents is principal, and their principal product is square with a forbidden 3 contribution. The witness \(\gamma\) therefore shows real class-group cancellation which does **not** correspond to a cubic parameter. This does not rule out nonsquare primitive ideals with other supports, or construct a reducible cubic.
