# Finite covers cannot add a norm identity and retain infinitely many integral Pell parameters

Consider either of the actual Pell conics
\[
 H^2-5Ap^2=R,\quad (A,R)=(1,10924)\ \text{or}\ (26,283599).
\]
The first has two genuine offsets \(K=10940,10928\); the second has three \(K=10940,10928,10908\). These come from [the original two-label construction](JOINT_LABEL_PELL_ORBIT.md) and [the common-projection triple](JOINT_TRIPLE_AND_CROSS_BASE_PELL_ORBITS.md). On neither curve can a finite cover defined over \(\mathbb Q\) both retain infinitely many rational points over distinct integral p and make any further actual offset \(5p^2+K\), \(K=10942-5^e-3^f\), a rational-function sum of two squares. This includes even-degree covers and arbitrary rational coordinate directions on the cover.

## Infinite integral images force a power map

Let \(D=5A\). On the base, integrality of p and rationality of H imply H is integral. On a fixed finite cover, points lying above these integral points are integral away from a fixed finite set S after choosing an affine model on the inverse image of the affine base (extend the finite map over \(\mathbb Z[S^{-1}]\)). Pass to a smooth normalization; an infinite set of p values is unaffected by omitting finitely many exceptional fibers. By [Siegel's theorem](https://www.numdam.org/item/CRMATH_2002__334_4_267_0.pdf) infinite such points require genus zero and at most two geometric poles of p. The quadratic conic has two poles, so there is precisely one geometric pole above each. The cover has a rational anchor and is thus rational of genus zero. Over \(F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt D)\), use \(u=H+\sqrt Dp\) with other coordinate \(R/u\). The two poles of p are \(u=0,\infty\) and both are rational over F. Each corresponding preimage on the cover is likewise F-rational, by uniqueness. A coordinate t on the cover can therefore take these two preimages to 0 and infinity, and take the chosen affine anchor to 1. The map has the forced form
\[
 u=u_0t^n,
\]
where the positive integer n is its degree and \(u_0\) is the anchor value. It is unramified at every point in the affine base. In particular, any additional fixed Pell condition \(H'^2-D'p^2=R'\), with \(D',R'\ne0\) and \(R'/D'\ne R/D\), introduces a square root branched at simple finite zeros, and cannot hold on an infinite set of common integer points (its normalization would be a ramified cover of this kind).

## A simple inert zero persists on every such cover

First take an additional positive K. At a geometric zero of \(5p^2+K\), set \(x=H\) and \(y=\sqrt Dp\). Then \(x^2=R-AK,\ y^2=-AK\), both nonzero for the offsets considered. For the base residue field
\[
 M_0=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{R-AK},\sqrt{-5K})
\]
the replay below confirms \(i\notin M_0\) for every additional positive K. Also there is a fixed prime q for each conic (2731 or 31511 respectively) such that \(v_q(R)=1\) and \(q\nmid 2D K(R-AK)\) for all these offsets. Work in the number field \(M_0F(i)\) at any place of q. This field is generated by square roots of q-adic units, and q is odd, so it is unramified there. The two quantities \(x+y,x-y\) are integral there, with product R. Their valuations are therefore 0 and 1. The two sign choices are permitted geometric points over \(M_0\), each having that same base residue field up to its chosen embedding. Choose the point for which \(v_q(u/u_0)=1\) or \(-1\); it exists because \(v_q(u_0)\) is 0 or 1: the integral anchor makes both \(u_0=H_0+\sqrt Dp_0\) and \(R/u_0=H_0-\sqrt Dp_0\) integral and their product is R. The polynomial \(t^n-u/u_0\), or the reciprocal equation when the valuation is \(-1\), is Eisenstein at this place. Thus the fiber remains irreducible of degree n even after extending the residue constants to \(M_0F(i)\). In particular it is irreducible over \(M_0(i)\), and its residue field over \(M_0\) does not contain i. The argument does not assume \(i\notin M_0F\); the stronger field is used only to check irreducibility.

At the chosen base point \(H=x\ne0,\ p\ne0\), the target has a simple zero, since p is a local coordinate and the derivative is \(10p\ne0\). That simple zero persists upstairs on the unramified cover. Since i is absent from the residue field, adjoining i is inert at this point, and a Gaussian norm has even valuation. This excludes the new rational-function identity. All actual positive K come from \(0\le e\le5,\ 0\le f\le8\), and no K in that box is zero. For any negative K, \(p=0\) is attained by a real point of the power cover: choose \(u=\operatorname{sgn}(u_0)\sqrt R\) and a positive real t. The target is then negative and cannot be a sum of squares of real rational functions, whose leading poles cannot cancel. This covers all remaining labels regardless of activation at particular positive integer images.

This theorem does not forbid isolated additional genuine norms (the two-label orbit already has a five-hit seed), nor infinitely many arithmetic points whose square coordinates do not form identities on any such fixed cover.

~~~javascript
function sq(x) {
  if (x<0n) return false;
  let l=0n,r=x+1n;
  while(r-l>1n){const t=(l+r)/2n;if(t*t>x)r=t;else l=t}
  return l*l===x;
}
function check(ok){if(!ok)throw Error("invalid certificate")}
for (const [A,R,q,old,expected] of [
  [1n,10924n,2731n,[10940n,10928n],52],
  [26n,283599n,31511n,[10940n,10928n,10908n],51]
]) {
  for(let d=2n;d*d<=q;d++) check(q%d!==0n);
  check(R%q===0n && R%(q*q)!==0n);
  let count=0;
  for(let e=0n;e<6n;e++) for(let f=0n;f<9n;f++){
    const K=10942n-5n**e-3n**f;
    check(K!==0n);
    if(K<0n||old.includes(K)) continue;
    count++;
    const x=R-A*K,y=-5n*K;
    check(x!==0n && 10n*A*K*x%q!==0n);
    check(![x,y,x*y].some(v=>sq(-v)));
  }
  check(count===expected);
}
~~~
