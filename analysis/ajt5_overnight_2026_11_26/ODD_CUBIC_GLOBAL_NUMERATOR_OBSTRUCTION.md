# A global numerator obstruction for the actual odd cubic pullback

In the [odd cubic criterion](CUBIC_PULLBACK_DOMAIN_AND_GENUS.md), let \(E=\mathbb Q(i,\delta)\), \(\delta^2=B=9633851\), \(m=4816930\), \(K=m^2-B\), and \(u=2+i(\delta-m)\). If \(z^2(z-1)=tu\) with \(z\in E,\ t\in\mathbb Q^\times\), the rational parameter in lowest terms must be \(t=A/D^3\), \(D>0\), with **nonsquarefree numerator A**, and with \((D,N_{E/\mathbb Q}(u))=1\). Both signs of squarefree numerator are excluded at every denominator height. No admissible cubic parameter with repeated numerator factors is asserted.

## Denominators and the nonreal relative norm

The absolute norm is \(N=(K+4)^2+16B=5^2\cdot17\cdot368793917\cdot3434854150155653\). All four primes split completely: at 5 and 17 this is explicit below; for the other two the norm exponent one gives a place of residue degree one, and the biquadratic Galois field is unramified there. The valuations of u across the places are 0 or 1, with both values represented. If \(v_p(t)<0\) at such a prime, the valuation at a unit component must be a negative multiple of 3, whereas at a valuation-one component the exponent differs by one and remains negative. This is impossible since the left side has valuation \(3v(z)\) when \(v(z)<0\). At any other prime with \(v_p(t)<0\), the same valuation identity forces \(3\mid v_p(t)\), since the ramification index in E is a power of two. Thus write \(t=A/D^3\), and \(w=Dz\) satisfies \(w^2(w-D)=Au\). It is integral by this monic equation. The ideals \((w)\) and \((w-D)\) are coprime: a common prime would divide D, where Au is a unit.

Put \(\eta=i\delta\), \(F^-=\mathbb Q(\eta)\). The relative norm \(N_{E/F^-}(z)\) cannot be rational. Otherwise for \(\theta=z/\bar z\) and \(\sigma\) flipping \(\delta\) while fixing i, \(1=N_{E/F^-}(\theta)=\theta/\sigma\theta\), so \(\theta\in\mathbb Q(i)\). Real z cannot match the equation's nonreal RHS. A pure imaginary \(z=iv\) would force \(v^2=2t\) and \(v^3\) proportional to \(\delta-m\), impossible since \(v\in\mathbb Q(\delta)\) with rational square lies on one of the two rational coordinate axes. In all other cases \(z=(1+ij)v\) for \(j\in\mathbb Q^\times,\ v\in\mathbb Q(\delta)^\times\). Comparing real and imaginary parts yields, with
\[
 U=(1-3j^2)(\delta-m)-2j(3-j^2),\quad
 V=-4j-(j^2-1)(\delta-m),
\]
the identities \(v=V/U,\quad t=j(1+j^2)^2V^2/U^3\). Here \(U\ne0\): the \(\delta\) coefficient \(1-3j^2\) is nonzero for rational j. Rationality requires the \(\delta\) coefficient in \(V^2(U^\sigma)^3\) to vanish. It is an integer polynomial of degree 13 with leading coefficient \(-16m\). The three finite values modulo 3 are \(2,1,1\) at \(j=0,1,-1\), and the homogeneous value at infinity modulo 3 is 2. Thus it has no rational root.

## Squarefree numerator contradiction

Assume A squarefree. At unramified rational primes, the coprime ideal equation implies \((w)\) can contain only primes dividing u and A, each to exponent at most one. At 2 the field \(F^-\) is inert (its discriminant is \(-B\equiv5\bmod8\)), so the 2-parts of its relative norm are removed by factoring out the integer content. At \(173,233,239\) the field \(F^-\) is ramified; E is obtained by adjoining i. The former two split in this relative extension and the last has residue degree two. Each upstairs exponent in \((w)\) is at most one by squarefree A and the rational ramification index two. Upon taking \(N_{E/F^-}(w)\) and removing integer content, the first two primes can contribute at most one exponent to its norm, and the last contributes none. Therefore the nonreal primitive element \(n_0\) obtained from this relative norm has absolute norm
\[
 M=5^e17^f173^g233^h(368793917)^j(3434854150155653)^\ell ,
 \qquad 0\le e\le2,\quad f,g,h,j,\ell\in\{0,1\}.
\]
The replay of all 96 possible M gives only \(M=N\) and \(n_0=\pm(K+4\pm4\eta)\). To justify completeness, write the element as \((x+y\eta)/2\) with \(x,y\) of the same parity and \(x^2+By^2=4M\); for nonreal elements take \(y>0\) up to conjugation. Here \(M\) is odd, so primitivity gives \((y,M)=1\), and \(x/y\) belongs to one of the listed complete CRT square-root classes of \(-B\bmod M\). For each class use the lattice generated by \((M,0),(r,1)\), Gauss-reduced for the norm \(x^2+By^2\). Every nonzero vector has norm at least M by congruence. The reduced vectors make angle with absolute cosine at most \(1/2\), so a vector of norm \(4M\) has both integer coefficients of absolute value at most \(\sqrt{16/3}<3\). This is a complete short-vector enumeration.

The norm \(M=N\) forces every prime of \((u)\) to occur once in \((w)\): each unramified exponent at a prime of N is at most the corresponding exponent of \((u)\) before removing content, and the norm is maximal only if none is missed. At 173 and 233 the two upstairs exponents are equal because their sum has even parity after content removal. The unique primes at 239 and at 2 are fixed by complex conjugation. Thus \((w/u)\) is stable under complex conjugation. Its ratio to its conjugate is a unit all of whose embeddings have absolute value one, hence a root of unity. The biquadratic E has only \(\{\pm1,\pm i\}\) as roots of unity, by its three quadratic subfields. Consequently \(z=\lambda v u\) with \(v\in\mathbb Q(\delta)^\times\) and \(\lambda\in\{1,i,1+i,1-i\}\).

Write \(Y=\delta-m\). Solving the imaginary part of \(t=\lambda^3v^3u^2-\lambda^2v^2u\) and substituting back, the four respective expressions for t are
\[
 -\frac{Y^2+4}{64},\quad
 \frac{2Y^2(Y^2+4)}{(4-Y^2)^3},\quad
 \frac{8(Y+2)(Y^2+4)}{(Y^2+4Y-4)^3},\quad
 -\frac{8(Y-2)(Y^2+4)}{(Y^2-4Y-4)^3}.
\]
In the residue field \(\mathbb F_9\) at 3, \(Y^2\equiv\delta\). The first three expressions reduce to \(-1-\delta,2\delta,-1-\delta\), respectively. For the last expression, \((Y^2-4Y-4)/3\equiv2\delta\bmod3\), using \(B\equiv8,m\equiv4\bmod9\); thus \(27t\equiv1+\delta\). In every case a 3-adically integral rational quantity would have residue in \(\mathbb F_3\), a contradiction. This excludes squarefree A.

## No proper rational intermediate quotient retaining t

The previously computed genus-21 curve has geometric monodromy \(S_3^4\), with point stabilizer \(H=C_2^4\). Every intermediate subgroup containing H is obtained by enlarging a subset of the factors from \(C_2\) to \(S_3\). Indeed if an element of the subgroup has a coordinate outside that \(C_2\), conjugation moves the stabilizer transposition supported on that coordinate and the two transpositions generate the whole coordinate \(S_3\). The four distinct nonzero branch values correspond to the conjugates of u and are transitively permuted over \(\mathbb Q\), so no nonempty proper subset descends. Hence there is no proper rational intermediate curve over the t-line. Maps not factoring the t projection are outside this result.
