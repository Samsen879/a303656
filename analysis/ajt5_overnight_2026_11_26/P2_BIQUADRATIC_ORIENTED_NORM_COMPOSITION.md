# Exact biquadratic composition cost for two oriented norm factors

I obtained an exact two-norm composition theorem. In \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3},\sqrt{-5})\), the minimum cost of combining two oriented edge factors is determined prime by prime. A prime that splits on its own edge, is inert in the other quadratic field, and occurs to odd depth costs exactly one extra copy of that prime at the ideal level. A class-group test then gives the exact cost for an integral generator.

### Exact composition theorem

Let \(K_3=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\), \(K_5=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-5})\), and \(F=K_3K_5\). Take primitive integral ideals \(I_3,I_5\) with coprime norms \(D,C\), both prime to \(30\). For this statement each input is supported on split rational primes in its own quadratic field and uses at most one of the two primes above each rational prime. Thus the orientation is specified. The prime-to-30 parts of the primitive norm factors used here meet that condition.

Require an integral ideal of \(F\) whose relative ideal norms are divisible by \(I_3\) and \(I_5\). Define
\[
\lambda=
\prod_{\substack{p^j\parallel D\\ j\ {\rm odd}\\(-5/p)=-1}}p
\prod_{\substack{p^j\parallel C\\ j\ {\rm odd}\\(-3/p)=-1}}p,
\qquad M=\lambda DC.
\]
Then **\(M\) is the least ideal norm**, and it divides every admissible norm. The least norm of an integral element meeting the same requirements is
\[
\boxed{\displaystyle M\quad\text{if }(M/5)=1,\qquad 3M\quad\text{if }(M/5)=-1.}
\]

At a prime of an input ideal, the relative residue degree is one if the prime splits in the other field and two if it is inert there. The latter case rounds the demanded depth up to the next even integer. Coprimality lets these choices be made separately at every rational prime.

For the generator claim, the tensor basis from \(\omega=(1+\sqrt{-3})/2\) and \(s=\sqrt{-5}\) has discriminant \(3600\), so the Minkowski bound for \(F\) is below \(10\). Only primes of norms \(3,4,7\) are needed to generate its class group. The unique prime of norm \(4\) is principal, generated by \(\sqrt{-3}+s\). The element \(1+s\), of norm \(36\), makes the square of one prime of norm \(3\) principal. The two primes of norm \(3\) are inverse classes by the factorization of \((\sqrt{-3})\). The element \(1+\omega s\), of norm \(21\), and its conjugates put all four primes of norm \(7\) in that same class. A prime of norm \(3\) is nonprincipal because its relative norm to \(K_5\) has norm \(3\), which \(x^2+5y^2\) cannot represent. Thus the relative norm induces an isomorphism between the two class groups of order two. In \(K_5\), a split prime ideal is principal when \(p\) is a square modulo \(5\); in the other class, multiplying by the prime above \(2\) gives a generator of norm \(2p\). This gives the test \((M/5)\). If the class is nontrivial, an ideal of norm \(3\) corrects it, and there is no ideal of norm \(2\). [Minkowski bound and ideal norms](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/ANT.pdf)

For any putative ordinary match with \(v=H\ell\), composing the two short factors costs at least \(p\). If the construction composes both short and long pairs separately, the odd total \(q\)-depth forces a charge on at least one pair, so the product of the two norm multipliers is at least \(pq=1328914076021502833\). For the actual oriented divisors with norms \(p,q^3\), the bound is attained at \(p^2q^4\) by
\[
(2612+1105\sqrt{-3})(120686845382+17302650345\sqrt{-5}).
\]
These two fixed primes give a concrete cofinal lower bound on permitted inflation in this composition model, ruling out multipliers less than the stated constants, but not arbitrary bounded inflation. The formula also charges moving primes whenever they occur with the specified depths and splitting types. It does not show that their product grows along the unrestricted pinned family.

For a prescribed integer \(n\), this composition applies once exact edges are available at a common \(K=n-t^2\). It neither synchronizes the edge sums nor supplies the required shared coordinate \(t\). Its obstruction is to combining the oriented norm factors at a prescribed cost. The original single-label existence question remains open.

The three quadratic subfield discriminants are −3, −20 and 60; their product gives discriminant 3600 of the biquadratic field. The prime of norm 2 cannot appear in this field because 2 is already inert in the −3 subfield. In the control at q, the squared-oriented factor 120686845382+17302650345√−5 is (497417+34785√−5)²/2; dividing by the factor above two removes only its ramified 2-part, and preserves the q orientation. These costs do not force growth of a prime-dependent penalty at unrestricted matched heights.
