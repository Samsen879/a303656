# A bounded tight-pair core for private roots

Fix an integer and a selected finite set Q of primes covering the activity in all the considered genuine parent blocks. A row is covered only by a finite odd valuation. For this note a pair of blocks is tight if the exact minimum number of covering primes for their union equals |Q|. Form the simple graph of tight pairs on the parent blocks. Fix a color q in Q. A private root of q is a numerical root z=5^e+3^f whose active residual is covered by q and by no other member of Q. Every tight pair contains a private root of q, since otherwise Q without q still covers the pair.

A root belongs to at most four parent blocks: at least one summand lies in [z/2,z], and each base has at most one power in this interval. Its choice determines the other exponent, so there are at most two exponent labels per numerical root. A child exponent e has at most two adjacent parent blocks. Consequently s specified private roots certify q on every tight pair exactly when the union of their block memberships is a vertex cover of the tight-pair graph, and that union has at most 4s vertices.

**Finite constraint core.** For any simple graph and nonnegative k, there is a subset of at most binomial(k+2,2) edges imposing exactly the same vertex-cover conditions on sets of size at most k as the original graph. Retain an inclusion-minimal subset with that property. For each retained edge e there is a set S_e of size at most k disjoint from e but intersecting all other retained edges. In a uniform random permutation, the event that both endpoints of e precede every element of S_e has probability 1/binomial(|S_e|+2,2). Two such events cannot coexist: each edge meets the other edge's witness set, forcing one chosen pair of vertices to precede each other. Summing probabilities proves the bound.

At k=4s this gives a core of at most binomial(4s+2,2) tight pairs to test whether s private roots suffice for a color. In particular s=1 needs at most 15 constraints, independent of the number of other colors and parent blocks. The core bound is on the **constraints**, not on the number of private roots necessary. Shared labels are collapsed by numerical root in this argument; attributing one shared row independently to two blocks can overstate actual reuse. This uses a standard set-pairs argument, with no novelty claim, and supplies no global absence of a covering.
