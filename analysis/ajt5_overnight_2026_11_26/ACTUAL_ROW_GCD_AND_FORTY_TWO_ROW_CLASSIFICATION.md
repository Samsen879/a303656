# Actual-row gcd compression and a complete 42-row norm classification

For four strictly positive actual row values \(A,B,C,D\), write \((A,B)=g(a,b)\) and \((C,D)=h(c,d)\) using their respective gcds. If \(bd=t^2\), let \(G=\gcd(a,c)\), \(U=a/G,V=c/G\), and \(S=ghtG\). Then \(ABCD=S^2 UV\) and \(\gcd(U,V)=1\), so the product is a two-square norm precisely when both \(U,V\) are. Multiplying their Gaussian coordinates and scaling by \(S\) constructs integer coordinates for the full product. Zero rows are excluded.

At \(n=3^{2H}+1\), \(d=0\), set \(X_j=3^{2H-j}(3^j-1)\). For odd \(H\ge u>v\ge1\) (only \(u,v\) need be odd), on the two pairs \(X_{2u},X_u\) and \(X_{2v},X_v\) the remaining factors after per-pair gcd reduction are \(3^u+1,3^u\) and \(3^v+1,3^v\). Thus \(bd=3^{u+v}\) is square. Writing \(g_0=\gcd(u,v)\), the cross gcd is \(3^{g_0}+1\): both \(u/g_0,v/g_0\) are odd. When \(v\mid u\) the sole remaining quotient is
\[
Q=(3^u+1)/(3^v+1).
\]
At \((H,u,v)=(42,35,7)\) the four distinct actual exponent rows \(\{14,49,70,77\}\) give \(Q=(-1169155)^2+4636746^2\). Their product is a true norm but every nonempty proper subproduct fails by the independent inert tests at 3, 547, and 71, checked below. At the same target the \((15,3)\) quotient has exact odd valuation at 31. The \((7,1)\) quotient 547 fails the Gaussian test but equals \(23^2+2\cdot3^2\).

## Growing failure via actual factors

For odd \(r\) and \(x\equiv3\pmod4\), \(x\ge3\), put \(Q_r=(x^r+1)/(x+1)\). If \(Q_r\) is a two-square norm, \(r\) must itself be a norm, and every inert prime dividing \(r\) must divide \(x+1\). This adapts the largest-inert-prime argument in [Dresden et al., Theorem 1.7 and its proof](https://msp.org/involve/2019/12-4/involve-v12-n4-p04-s.pdf) to a quotient, not a claim of novelty for the argument.

If \(r\) is not a norm choose its largest inert divisor \(p\) of odd valuation \(a\), and consider \(W=(x^{p^a}+1)/(x^{p^{a-1}}+1)\equiv3\pmod4\). The prime \(p\) occurs in \(W\) only when \(p\mid x+1\), with valuation one by LTE; then \(v_p(Q_r)=a\). Each other prime \(q\) dividing \(W\) has order \(2p^a\) at \(x\bmod q\): it cannot divide the denominator since the quotient polynomial would reduce to \(p\); thus \(q>p\). For inert \(q\ne p\), LTE gives \(v_q(Q_r)=v_q(W)+v_q(r)\), and \(v_q(r)\) is even by the choice of \(p\). Hence the full divisor of \(Q_r\) supported on \(W\) is \(3\bmod4\), and is coprime to its complementary factor. It is a checkable nonnorm certificate without factoring the large \(W\). If \(r\) is a norm, use the same computation for any inert \(p\mid r\) not dividing \(x+1\), and all parity transfers at the inert primes of \(W\) still hold. With \(u=21v\), \(v\) odd and \(H=u\), this gives a growing negative four-row product, while the fixed pair \((u,v)=(5,1)\) gives quotient 61 on the same actual target.

## Entire 42-row even-exponent domain

At \(H=42\) the even positive rows are \(R_k=9^{42}-9^{42-k}\), \(1\le k\le42\). Group them into chains indexed by the odd part of \(k\). An arbitrary subset has norm product if and only if it has even cardinality in every chain other than the chains with odd parts \(1,5,13\).

Indeed, every \(k,2k\) edge is generated by the Gaussian coordinates of \(9^k+1\) after scaling by \(3^{84-3k}(9^k-1)\). The three named chains have individual norm anchors. The resulting 24 independent generators give exactly the stated chain-parity space. Sums of generator support masks correspond to multiplying norm products and removing repeated rows as squares; this preserves normhood by the inert valuation test. Independently, 18 distinct inert prime valuation characters have rank 18 on the 42 actual rows. The two dimensions sum to 42, proving the converse without enumerating all menus. The exact number of menus is \(2^{24}\); the maximum size is 30, with respectively 9, 108, 699 choices of size 30, 29, 28. This classifies only the stated even-row domain. The four-row relation above uses odd rows as well; none of these products asserts that the individual rows represent the original target.
