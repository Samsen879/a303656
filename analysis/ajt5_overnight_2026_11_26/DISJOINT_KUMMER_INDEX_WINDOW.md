# A GRH-only disjoint-factor window for the two index axes

In the cubic panel write $I_9=(p-1)/\operatorname{ord}_p(9)$, $I_{25}=(p-1)/\operatorname{ord}_p(25)$ and $U=\operatorname{ord}_p(9)\operatorname{ord}_p(25)$. Fix $C\ge2$, $L=\log Y$, $Z=Y^{1/8}/\sqrt L$. Consider primes $Y<p\le2Y$ with $\gcd(I_9,I_{25})\le C$ and with a prime $q\in[Z,2Z]$ dividing $I_9$ and a prime $r\in[3Z,6Z]$ dividing $I_{25}$. Under GRH for the relevant Kummer Dedekind zeta functions, there are $O(Y^{3/4}/L^2)$ such primes. For the part with $U\ge Y^{9/8}$, this bounds $\sum U^{-2/3}$ by $O(L^{-2})$.

Indeed for sufficiently large $Y$, both cross-divisibilities $q\mid I_{25}$ and $r\mid I_9$ must be absent. For fixed distinct $q,r$, the required positive divisibilities are complete splitting in $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{qr},9^{1/q},25^{1/r})$. The degree is $\varphi(qr)qr$. For the two cross conditions respectively adjoin the missing $q$th root of 25 or $r$th root of 9; those enlarge the degree by $q$ or $r$, and by $qr$ if both are adjoined. To see the relative degrees, work over $\mathbb Q(\zeta_{qr})$: the valuations at primes over 3 and 5 show independence between the two powers for each prime exponent, and the extensions of relatively prime degree are disjoint. Inclusion–exclusion now gives main coefficient $(1-1/q)(1-1/r)/[\varphi(qr)qr]=1/(q^2r^2)$, not a product of unrestricted marginal events.

The GRH complete-splitting error for each of the four fields is $O(\sqrt Y\log(Yqr))$ by the normalized root-discriminant bound; see [Sgobba, Proposition 13 and section 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.05417v2#S4). Each main term and error are $O(\sqrt Y L)$, and there are $O(Y^{1/4}/L^3)$ prime pairs, giving the asserted count. More generally the same bound controls $U\ge Y^{9/8}/L^\lambda$ for fixed $\lambda<3$. Treating the field errors separately gives main and error powers $Y^{1-\delta+o(1)}$ and $Y^{1/2+\delta+o(1)}$ when the two window scales have product $Y^\delta$. Balancing them yields $Y^{3/4+o(1)}$, so this particular error summation does not reach fixed $U\asymp Y^\theta$ layers with $\theta<9/8$. It does not rule out a better averaged estimate, and does not address primes whose indices lack factors in the prescribed windows.
