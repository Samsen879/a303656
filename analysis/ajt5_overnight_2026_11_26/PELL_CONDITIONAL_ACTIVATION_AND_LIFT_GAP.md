# Conditional Pell activation and the separate valuation-lift gap

This note gives a **conditional** positive-density theorem for actual mod-prime activations on the \(K=10936\) Pell track. It does not give a density or an unconditional existence theorem for parity-changing lifts. The GRH stated here is the full sufficient range of the cited theorem; no minimal GRH range is claimed.

Fix \(L\ge1\). Write \(\sigma=387860,\ \phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2,\ G=\phi^{6\sigma},\ E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5,\sqrt{-3},\sqrt{-2734})\), and
\[
 \beta=(2\sqrt{-3}+2\sqrt{-2734})/(3577+1599\sqrt5).
\]
Put \(f=72\prod r\), where \(r\) runs through the distinct odd prime divisors other than 3 of \(1367\sigma L\). We seek degree-one primes of \(E\) over rational primes \(q\) satisfying \(q\equiv3\bmod8,\ q\equiv4\bmod9,\ q\equiv-1\bmod r\) for each such \(r\), with \(\bar\beta\) a sixth power and the residual index of \(\phi\) dividing six at the same place.

Use [Lenstra's Theorems 3.1 and 4.6](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1977d/art.pdf) with base \(E\), subgroup \(\langle\phi\rangle\), allowed index divisor \(6\), and auxiliary field \(H=\mathbb Q(\zeta_f)E(\beta^{1/6})\). In the theorem's notation take the least prime power \(e_\ell\) not dividing 6, so \(e_2=4,e_3=9\), and \(e_\ell=\ell\) otherwise. Assume GRH for the Dedekind zeta functions of the composita of \(E(\zeta_{e_\ell},\phi^{1/e_\ell})\) for every finite set of \(\ell\), including the empty set. This is the squarefree-index range in the original theorem, not a hypothesis about \(q^2\) lifting.

Here is the finite-place compatibility check. The prescribed cyclotomic element fixes \(E\), by the quadratic characters for \(5,-3,-2734\): the last character factors into those for \(2,-1367\), both of value \(-1\) on the chosen class. In particular \(E\subset\mathbb Q(\zeta_f)\). The cyclic degree-six extension \(E(\beta^{1/6})/E\) has one place over 2731 where \(\beta\) has valuation one and another where \(\beta\) is a unit, as checked by \((\sqrt5,\sqrt{-3},\sqrt{-2734})\equiv(1220,893,\pm893)\). Every nontrivial subextension is fully ramified at the first place and unramified at the second. In any rational abelian extension containing \(E\) the inertia subgroups at these conjugate places coincide, so the intersection is just \(E\). Thus the specified cyclotomic element and the identity on \(E(\beta^{1/6})\) give a single element of \(\operatorname{Gal}(H/E)\).

There is no positive power obstruction over \(E\) for \(\phi\): for odd prime exponents, use the norm \(E/\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\) of degree four, and the fundamental-unit property in \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\); for squares, the real field \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5,\sqrt\phi)\) has a nonreal rational conjugate embedding (the conjugate of \(\phi\) is negative). It therefore is not contained in the abelian extension \(E/\mathbb Q\). For \(\ell\ge5\) the field \(E(\zeta_\ell,\phi^{1/\ell})/E\) is nonabelian: \(\phi\) remains a non-\(\ell\)-th-power in \(E(\zeta_\ell)\) by a coprime-degree norm, and the nontrivial cyclotomic action on the cyclic root subgroup is nontrivial. Here \(\zeta_\ell\notin E\), since \(E/\mathbb Q\) has exponent two and \(\ell-1\ge4\). These obstruction fields are not contained in abelian \(H/E\). The obstruction fields for 2 and 3, if contained in \(H\), are already avoided by the action on \(\zeta_4,\zeta_9\). These checks are the finite compatibility required by Lenstra. It follows under the stated GRH that the desired set of primes of \(E\) has positive natural density. Higher residue degrees contribute negligibly to prime ideal counts by norm, so there are degree-one primes of positive density in that count.

For such a place \(M=(q-1)/6\) is coprime to \(6\sigma L\); \(\overline{G^L}\) generates the sixth-power subgroup. Thus \(\beta\) is actually attained by its powers, and shifting any class \(n=a+Lt\) only multiplies the target by \(G^{-a}\) inside this subgroup. This concludes the mod-\(q\) activation result.

For parity, however, put \(h=v_q(\phi^{q-1}-1)\) and \(b=v_q(\beta^{q-1}-1)\) at the same place. The local certificate in [Odd-valuation escapes](../ajt5_integer_certificates/PELL_ODD_VALUATION_ESCAPES_FIXED_MENUS.md) requires \(b\ge h\); if \(b<h\), the activated phase has constant valuation \(b\). The mod-prime density theorem does not bound this bad set. A single prime passing \(b\ge h\) for each fixed \(L\) would suffice to vary the pivot parity in every prescribed class at that \(L\) and, by the existing menu-freezing argument, force a new inert prime. It would not take a positive density of parity-changing primes. No such single-prime supply for arbitrary \(L\) has been proved here.

## Unconditional common-index exclusion at 2731

The prime used in the finite-field argument cannot itself supply a real odd valuation on the constrained integer curve: for all integer \(X,Y\),
\[
 X^2-5Y^2=10924,\quad41\mid Y
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad2731\nmid X^2+12 .
\]
In \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)\), 2 is inert and 2731 splits. As \(N(\alpha)=4\cdot2731\), the ideal of any element of norm \(10924\) is the ideal of \(\alpha\) or \(\bar\alpha\): the factor over 2 is forced and the split prime allows one of two choices. Their quotients are norm-one units, so the elements are \(\pm\alpha\phi^{2k}\) or \(\pm\bar\alpha\phi^{2k}\).

Modulo 41 in the branch \(\sqrt5=13\), both seed components equal 10, and the two components of \(\phi^2\) are 8 and its inverse. The order of 8 is 20, so equality of the two components, required by \(41\mid Y\), forces \(10\mid k\). Modulo 2731, for \(\alpha\) use the nonzero branch \(\sqrt5=1220\): \(\alpha=1692,\ \bar\alpha=0,\ \phi^2=1977\) of order 195. Here \(X^2+12=0\) demands \(1977^k=\pm609\), since the two components of \(X+Y\sqrt5\) are one zero and \(2X\). Direct powering gives \(1977^6=609\), and the negative is not in the odd-order subgroup. Thus the phase is \(k=6\bmod195\); for the conjugate seed the phase is \(-6\bmod195\). Neither is compatible with \(5\mid k\).

Without the \(41\) constraint, \((X,Y)=(1151537,514983)\) is an actual integer Pell point with \(v_{2731}(X^2+12)=1\), as direct integer division confirms. It and the original seed give simultaneous solutions to any finite prescribed \(2731\)-power and \(41\)-power conditions by ordinary coordinate CRT, but not an integer point on the exact curve with both properties. The obstruction is the shared unit index.
