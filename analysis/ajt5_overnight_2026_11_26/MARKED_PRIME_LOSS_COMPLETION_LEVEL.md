# Marked-prime reciprocal losses and the completion level

Keep the legal row \(C=49g,\ X_g=B/g\), sharp target window and fixed shift from the [reciprocal sieve](https://github.com/Samsen879/a303656/commit/234605782deb0a63a4fd2014338c5d1ac729b04e). The literal classes \(s=ru\) with \(t,u,v,\lambda,r\) prime and \(v=ru\) with \(t,s,u,\lambda,r\) prime are part of the loss from replacing the prime tests by the sieve weight. Here \(u>r>z=B^{1/20000}\), \((r,14C\kappa\lambda)=1\), and the mark \(r\) is unique within either class. Their original sieve weight is one and the original prime triple indicator is zero.

Fix \(g,\lambda,r\) and a dyadic \(t\)-bin. In the first class the remaining reciprocal coordinates obey \(tu=\kappa(Cr)^{-1}\bmod\lambda\) and the sharp region has area \(O(X_g/r)\). In the second they obey \(ts=\kappa C^{-1}\bmod\lambda r\) and have area \(O(X_g)\). Their main terms are of order \(X_g/(\lambda r)\), with the same digit coefficient. In each case the local squarefree \(d\)-test still has the three-form density from the previous sieve, since \(r\) is a unit at the primes in the divisor expansion. The exact digit has 42 residue pairs modulo 196, including the invertible change from \(s\) to \(u\) in the first switch.

There are \(O(d3^{\omega(d)})\) local residue pairs to complete. Divide the sharp \(t\)-region into \(J\) strips. For the prime modulus the completion error per rectangle and progression pair is \(O(\sqrt\lambda\log^2 B)\). For the composite modulus, CRT gives \(O(\sqrt{\lambda r}\log^2 B)\) for the pairs whose two local frequency pairs are each nonzero. When both frequencies vanish at one prime, complete at the other; the two zero coefficients are excluded together as the principal pair. The interval lengths are \(O(\lambda)\), so the degenerate nonprincipal cases fit the same bound. Summing the divisor expansion to \(D=B^\alpha\) costs \(O(D^2\log^{O(1)} B)\) in the completion term when its multiplicities are bounded by \(3^{\omega(d)}\). Hence the relative error, up to logarithms, is
\[
J^{-1}+J D^2(\lambda^{3/2}/X_g)\begin{cases} r,&s=ru,\\r^{3/2},&v=ru.\end{cases}
\]
With \(\mu=\log_B(X_g/\lambda^{3/2})\) and \(r=B^\eta\), a power choice of \(J\) makes these errors small if \(\eta+2\alpha<\mu\) or \(3\eta/2+2\alpha<\mu\), respectively. This is the ceiling of this specific absolute-error estimate, not a general impossibility theorem for completion.

The uniform margin is \(\mu\ge301/384000-o(1)\). Taking \(D=z^2\), \(J=\lceil B^{1/40000}\rceil\) makes the marked **sieve** comparison power-small over \(B^{1/10000}<r\le B^{1/2000}\) in the first switch and \(B^{1/10000}<r\le B^{1/3000}\) in the second. The equal main term cancels over the seven digits. For the **literal** classes the upper sieve gives \(O(X_gF(g\kappa)/(\lambda r\log^3B))\) per fixed \(\lambda,r,t\)-bin within those ranges. Summing the full \(t,\lambda\) bands and \(b_*\) dyadic \(r\)-bins gives \(O(b_*B\mathcal H/\log^3 B)\). This is negligible at the secondary scale if \(b_*=o(L/\log L)\); it does not estimate literal signed cancellation across a power-wide \(r\)-range.

For example the maximal \(v\)-marker exponent in this completion with \(D=z^2\) is below \(0.00039\) at the tight margin. A least-prime detection of a composite \(v\) can require exponent \(1/4+o(1)\) even at the top of the \(\lambda\) band. Also, for fixed \(\lambda,r\) the short reciprocal coordinates give a partial matching. Deleting edges by literal prime restrictions or giving them digit phases does not change the Euclidean operator norm (one for a nonempty matching). Neither route supplies the remaining full signed loss bound.
