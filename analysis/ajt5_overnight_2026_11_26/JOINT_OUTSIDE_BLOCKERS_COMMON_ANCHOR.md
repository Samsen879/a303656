# Joint full-box bounds for common-anchor outside blockers

In the full active box of ordinary exponents \(3^c+5^d<n\), let \(L=\log n\). A prime blocks a cell if the positive residual has odd valuation at it. Consider primes \(q\equiv3\bmod4\) dividing the *same* anchor residual \(n-4\). Their product divides \(n-4\), so \(\sum_q\log q\le L\). This enables union bounds without any assumption about independence of different primes.

**Moderate band.** Use all such primes with \(L^{3/2}\le q\le3L^{3/2}\) and \(r=\operatorname{ord}_q3,\ s=\operatorname{ord}_q5\) at least \(L^{3/4}/8\). Their joint blocked fraction is \(O(1/\log L)\).

**Large band with no order test.** For \(3/7<\beta<1\), all such primes \(q>\exp(L^\beta)\) together block at most
\[
 O\!\left(L^{1-7\beta/3}+L^{-\beta}\right)
\]
of the active box, which tends to zero. Both bounds also hold on any fixed positive-proportion subset, with the constant enlarged by the reciprocal of that proportion.

For the proof, ignore the extra requirement of odd valuation for the upper bound and count all hits modulo \(q\). If either order is at least \(L/4\), fixing the other axis leaves \(O(1)\) exponent choices per row or column, so the fraction is \(O(1/L)\). If both orders are smaller, the period \(r\times s\) has \(O((rs)^{1/3})\) hits by [Mit'kin's lemma, stated as Lemma 10 by Shkredov, Solodkova and Vyugin](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1302.3839#page=10). Indeed \(n\equiv4\bmod q\) gives a nonzero target and a single pair of cosets. The size hypotheses are \((rs)^2<q^3\) and \(rs\ge27\); these hold for sufficiently large \(n\) in either band when the two orders are below \(L/4\) (in the large band use the elementary lower bounds \(r\ge\log(q+1)/\log3,\ s\ge\log(q+1)/\log5\)). Packing complete periods with end effects costs only a constant because the ordinary exponent sides are of order \(L\). The resulting per-prime fraction is \(O((rs)^{-2/3})\). In the moderate band \(rs\gg L^{3/2}\) gives \(O(1/L)\) and the common product permits only \(O(L/\log L)\) primes. In the large band \(rs\gg L^{2\beta}\) and there are at most \(L^{1-\beta}\) primes. This yields both terms above. The number of active cells used in the denominator is \(\Theta(L^2)\): all but the last possible exponent on both sides certainly give a positive residual.

For a finite exact check (not in the asymptotic moderate band), take \(n=1165706944821995580\). The factorization of \(n-4\) is \(2^3\cdot7\cdot11\cdot13\cdot19\cdot7661463173813\), the final factor prime \(1\bmod4\) (trial division suffices). The exact outside anchor primes relative to the panel \(q\le\sqrt{\log n}\) are \(7,11,19\). Enumerating the positive cells gives 988 total, 250 modulo-prime union hits, and 220 actual odd-valuation blocks by that union. The panel here consists of 3; requiring even valuation at 3 as well leaves 763 cells. Direct integer division includes the two valuation-three cells at 7 and the one at 11. No claim is made that an unblocked cell is a true norm, nor that unrelated outside primes are controlled.

## Pair bound for a selected two-anchor large-order-factor family

Put \(z=\sqrt{\log n}\), and take \(K\) selected primes \(z<q\le5z,\ q\equiv3\bmod4\) dividing \((n-4)(n-10)\). Require that each of the two true orders at a prime \(q\) contain the same prime factor \(\ell_q>z^{11/20}\), with distinct \(\ell_q\) for distinct selected \(q\). In the full ordinary active box, the fractions hit by at least one and two selected primes with odd valuation are, respectively,
\[
 O(K z^{-11/15}),\qquad O(K^2 z^{-22/15}).
\]
In particular, if \(K=O(z^{11/20})\), these are \(O(z^{-11/60})\) and \(O(z^{-11/30})\). The size restriction in this last conclusion is an explicit assumption. No existence theorem for such families with growing \(K\) is needed for the bounds.

For a nonzero target in a prime field and any two subgroup cosets of sizes \(r,s\), the solution count of \(x+y=\text{target}\) is \(O((rs)^{1/3})\) when \((rs)^2<q^3\), \(rs\ge27\), by the cited Mit'kin lemma. In the other large-product range use \(rs/q+\sqrt q\). To see the second bound, expand the two coset indicators as averages of multiplicative characters trivial on the corresponding subgroups, extending each character by zero at zero. The two trivial characters contribute \(rs(q-2)/(q-1)^2\le rs/q\); each other Jacobi sum is at most \(\sqrt q\) in absolute value, from Gauss-sum orthogonality (when a character or the product is trivial, its non-main sum has absolute value one). The weights in the average have total absolute mass one. In either case a period with \(r,s\ge z^{11/20}\) has hit fraction \(O(z^{-11/15})\).

Now fix two distinct selected primes with order pairs \((r_1,s_1),(r_2,s_2)\). Freeze the two exponent phases modulo \(g_r=\gcd(r_1,r_2)\) and \(g_s=\gcd(s_1,s_2)\). Neither large factor of one prime divides the other prime minus one: their product exceeds \(5z\) for large \(z\). Consequently the conditional subgroup cosets at the two primes, of orders \(r_i/g_r,s_i/g_s\), still have orders at least \(z^{11/20}\). Conditional on the frozen phases, the remaining period coordinates at the two primes are independent **by finite CRT**, since the pairs of quotient orders in each axis are coprime. The pair hit density in the full joint period is at most the product \(O(z^{-22/15})\). Both joint period sides are \(O(z^2)\), so packing the bounding exponent rectangle of side lengths \(\Theta(z^2)\) into periods, including partial ends, costs only a constant in density. The active box has size \(\Theta(z^4)\). Summing over single primes or pairs proves the bounds. Mod-\(q\) hits are used only as upper bounds for exact odd valuations.

In the finite integer \(n=1165706944821995580\) above, take the selected two-anchor primes \(7,11,23\) with order pairs \((6,6),(5,5),(11,22)\). Of the 988 positive ordinary cells, 16 are hit by at least two with odd valuation, checked by direct division. There are 491 cells passing even valuation at 3 and having odd part \(1\bmod4\), of which eight are double-hit by the selected primes. In the cell \((c,d)=(2,0)\), only 23 from the family has odd valuation; 79 outside the family also has exact valuation one. Hence looking only for a selected pair of blockers does not certify a true norm for that cell.

## Shared-factor blocks need not behave like one prime

For two primes, let \(a\) range over the two frozen exponent classes modulo their respective common axis-order gcds. Let \(\alpha_q(a)\) be the conditional *mod-prime* root density on the remaining fine coordinates of the period at \(q\). On the full joint period the pair root density is exactly \(\mathbb E_a\alpha_p(a)\alpha_q(a)\): the fine quotient periods are coprime after the coarse classes are fixed. With \(D_q=\max_a\alpha_q(a)\), \(\mu_q=\mathbb E_a\alpha_q(a)\), and \(\kappa\) the fraction of \(a\) in both supports, this gives
\[
 \delta_{p,q}\le\min\{D_p\mu_q,D_q\mu_p,\kappa D_pD_q\}.
\]
This exact identity does **not** say \(\delta_{p,q}=\mu_p\mu_q\). As before the weak-root density bounds actual odd-valuation hits from above, and the endpoints of the active box can be accounted for by period packing.

A block of primes \(z<q\le5z\) with a common prime \(\ell>z^{11/20}\) in both axis orders and with \(q\nmid n\) hits an \(O(z/\ell^{7/3}+1/\ell)\) fraction of the full active box. There are \(O(z/\ell+1)\) possible primes because each must be \(1\bmod\ell\); by the preceding Mit'kin/Jacobi dichotomy, the per-prime hit fraction is \(O(\ell^{-4/3}+1/z)\). Each period has side lengths \(O(z)\), so truncating to the full ordinary rectangle of sides \(\Theta(z^2)\) costs only a constant, including strict positivity in the denominator. Summing gives the displayed fraction, at most \(O(z^{-17/60})\). Thus a set already known to occupy a fixed positive fraction of active cells loses only \(o(1)\) of its own fraction to one such block. The estimate does not supply a positive proportion for an arbitrary prefiltered set and does not control arbitrarily many blocks.

In the 988-cell integer above the pair 7,19 has a shared order factor 3. Direct division gives odd-valuation marginal counts 123 and 46, with intersection 13, strictly more than the product-of-marginals prediction \(123\cdot46/988\). Of the 491 even-3, odd-part-\(1\bmod4\) cells, 416 remain after filtering this block. At \((c,d)=(8,4)\) the exact valuations at \(3,7,19\) are \(0,1,1\), and the odd part is \(1\bmod4\). Consequently requiring two distinct odd inert factors does not require two distinct blocks.

## A finite forest certificate using exact valuation-one overlaps

Let \(S\) be a specified set of positive residual cells and \(E_i\subseteq S\) the actual odd-valuation event for one prime or one block. For bounds \(U_i\ge |E_i|\), \(e_{ij}\le |E_i\cap E_j|\) and a forest \(F\) on the event indices, the number surviving every event is at least
\[
 |S|-\sum_i U_i+\sum_{ij\in F} e_{ij}.
\]
This is the pointwise forest form of [Hunter's union bound](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-applied-probability/article/abs/an-upper-bound-for-the-probability-of-a-union/092D711504BA968EF0D1D903A2685D60): a cell in \(m>0\) events contributes at most \(m-1\) induced forest edges. For singleton primes an edge can count only simultaneous valuation-one cells, a certified lower bound for simultaneous odd valuation. Intersection **upper** bounds cannot be used for the positive term.

An independent exact replay on \(n=1165706944821995580\) uses the 491 cells that have even valuation at 3 and odd part \(1\bmod4\). Take singleton events for inert primes from 7 through 1051571, dropping primes with no hit. There are 221 nonempty events with total size 491, so simple subtraction gives no guaranteed survivor. A maximum-weight forest using exact valuation-one overlaps contains 145 edges with total weight 168, so at least 168 survive. Exact filtering leaves 196. At \((c,d)=(7,11)\), all of 31,43,47 have exact valuation one; counting all three edges of that triangle would double-count the correction. Neither a forest survivor nor even a complete finite panel survivor need be a norm.

Replay: enumerate the ordinary exponents until \(3^c+5^d\ge n\), index the 491 cells, sieve the primes through 1051571, and directly divide each positive residual at each prime \(3\bmod4\). Record both the odd-valuation event list and the index list for valuation exactly one. For each cell, increment the weight of each pair of primes in the latter list by one. Process positive-weight edges in descending weight, breaking ties by increasing primes, and accept only edges joining different components. Adding the accepted weights gives the stated exact certificate relative to this panel.
