# Why the hidden-prime sublattice does not fill a continuous rectangle

Keep the CRT construction and its primes from ACTIVE_SUBLATTICE_EXACT_COVER.md. Let the actual exponents range over $0\le c\le C$, $0\le d\le D$, where $C,D\asymp L=\log X$. For hidden-prime period $T=\operatorname{lcm}_j q_j(q_j-1)$, its designated blocked sublattice occupies $(1+o(1))k/T^2$ of the box whenever $T=o(L)$: count each of the specified residues on its axis. Because $q_j$ avoids the divisors of $G$, the $k$ powers of $25$ at the anchors are distinct modulo $q_j$. They are nonzero squares, so $q_j\ge2k+1$. The product of the distinct $q_j$ divides $T$. For $k=\lfloor\log L/(20\log\log L)\rfloor$, this gives proportion at most $L^{-1/10+o(1)}$.

There is a stronger limit for a continuous rectangle covered by this entire certificate, including the original panel and auxiliary primes. Define $A,B$ to be the lcm of 2 and the respective orders of 3 and 5 over the hidden primes $q_j$. Every cell with $A\mid c$ and $B\mid d$ has residual congruent to $n-2$ at each hidden prime, hence is a unit there. Its exponents are even, so it is also a unit at each original/auxiliary inert prime, where $n\equiv0$; at $p=3$ the 5-power alone is nonzero unless $c=0$, in which case the sum of the two squares is still nonzero. Thus any continuous rectangle with at least $A$ consecutive $c$ positions and $B$ consecutive $d$ positions contains a survivor for all the certificate primes. A continuous rectangle weak-covered by those primes must have area fraction at most
$$
 \max\left(\frac{A-1}{C+1},\frac{B-1}{D+1}\right).
$$
The original hidden-prime bound $q_j=O(k^2\log k)$ gives $A,B\le\prod_j(q_j-1)\le L^{1/10+o(1)}$, so this area bound is $L^{-9/10+o(1)}$. This says nothing about coverage by other primes.

Indeed even such a certifiably unhit cell can have opposite actual norm behavior at two different integers with identical observed CRT phase. Choose one of its offsets $H=3^c+5^d>2$ in a large continuous rectangle contained in the $n$-independent strict box $3^c,5^d<X/4$. Then $H<X/2$. The fixed phase gives that $n-H$ is a unit at each observed prime, so one may CRT actual square coordinates for $n_+=H+u^2+v^2\in[X,2X)$, using the same modulus cost as before and choosing $u$ near $\sqrt{X-H}$. For the opposite integer choose an additional inert $q$ of size a sufficiently large constant times $L$, not dividing the original modulus or $H-2$. The fixed-progression prime number theorem ensures such a prime: the forbidden primes in a band of scale $CL$ number $O(L/\log L)$ from the size of the product, while their available count has leading coefficient proportional to $C$. Add $n_-\equiv H+q\bmod q^2$ and CRT square coordinates for $n_-=2+s^2+t^2\in[X,2X)$. Then the designated residual has valuation one at $q$, but the origin at $n_-$ is a true norm. The two constructions share the original observed phase. This is a comparison of two real integers, not a statement about every cell of one rectangle or the original conjecture.
