# Reversible biquadratic norm transfer and a rational recurrence barrier

**There is a reversible transfer for the full \(25^t-9^t\) norm, with one possible unit correction on the minus side.** It gives an actual construction from the two signed lifts. It also has a precise limitation. No fixed rational function of the two exponential bases can supply the plus lift at infinitely many points of an arithmetic progression.

### Reversible transfer

Let \(u^2=-3\), \(v_\pm^2=\pm5\), \(r_\pm=uv_\pm\), and put
\[
E=\mathbb Q(u),\qquad K_\pm=\mathbb Q(r_\pm),\qquad L_\pm=E(v_\pm).
\]
For odd \(t=2n+1\), use the previous signed \(\alpha_+\) and its real quadratic counterpart
\[
\alpha_+=\frac{5^{n+1}+(-1)^n3^{n+1}+(3^n-(-1)^n5^n)r_+}{8},
\qquad
\alpha_-=\frac{5^{n+1}-3^{n+1}+(3^n-5^n)r_-}{2}.
\]
Their norms are \(H_+=(5^t+3^t)/8\) and \(H_-=(5^t-3^t)/2\). Both are primitive. For the minus side this follows directly from \((5+r_-)\alpha_-=5^{n+1}+3^n r_-\), and \(\alpha_-\) is positive at both real embeddings.

For every odd \(t\), the following are equivalent:

- \(25^t-9^t=x^2+3y^2\) for integers \(x,y\).
- There are algebraic integers \(\beta_\pm\in L_\pm\) and \(j\in\{0,1\}\) with
  \[
  N_{L_+/K_+}\beta_+=\alpha_+,\qquad
  N_{L_-/K_-}\beta_-=(4+r_-)^j\alpha_-.
  \]

Given the lifts, the construction is to take
\[
x+yu=4\,N_{L_+/E}(\beta_+)\,N_{L_-/E}(\beta_-).
\]
The factor \(4\) ensures integral coordinates, and its norm is \(16\).

For the reverse direction, \(H_+\) and \(H_-\) are coprime odd integers. A full representation therefore supplies an \(E\)-ideal \(J_\pm\) of norm \(H_\pm\) for each half. The primitivity of \(\alpha_\pm\) selects one \(K_\pm\)-prime above each \(q\mid H_\pm\). If \(q\) splits in \(E\), lift the two exponents of \(J_\pm\) to the two \(L_\pm\)-primes above that selected prime. If \(q\) is inert in \(E\), its exponent in \(H_\pm\) is even, and half that exponent on the unique prime upstairs is the lift. This constructs a **unique ideal with the two prescribed ideal norms** \((\alpha_\pm)\) and \(J_\pm\).

These lifted ideals are principal. For \(L_+\), the discriminant bound \(225\) gives Minkowski bound \(<3\), and \(2\) is inert in \(E\), so no ideal has norm \(2\). The unit \((1+v_+)/2\) has relative norm \(-1\) and corrects the sign of the generator’s norm. For \(L_-\), the discriminant bound \(3600\) gives Minkowski bound \(<10\). The prime over \(2\) is principal, generated by \(u+v_-\) of norm \(4\). Primes over \(3\) have inverse classes, whose squares are principal by the factorization of \(1+v_-\). An element of norm \(21\), namely \(1+(1+u)v_-/2\), and its conjugates put all primes over \(7\) in those same classes. There are no other prime ideal norms below \(10\). The norm of a prime over \(3\) is nonprincipal in \(K_-\), since \(a^2-15b^2=\pm3\) is impossible modulo \(5\). Thus the norm on ideal classes is injective, and a lift with principal norm is principal. Its relative norm differs from \(\alpha_-\) by a totally positive unit, hence by a power of \(4+r_-\). Scaling by this unit changes the exponent by two, giving the stated \(j\). [Minkowski’s ideal-class bound](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~sharifi/notes/algnum-ch04.html)

### A barrier to a fixed recurrence construction

Suppose a fixed \(F(X,Y)\in L_+(X,Y)\) gave the plus lift \(F(5^m,3^m)\) at infinitely many indices with \(n=n_0+hm\), \(h>0\). Restrict to a parity subsequence so \(e=(-1)^n\) is fixed. Write the target as \(aX^h+bY^h\), where
\[
\frac ba=\left(\frac35\right)^{n_0}\frac{r_+}{5}.
\]
Distinct monomials in \(5^m,3^m\) have distinct exponential bases, so clearing denominators forces the rational-function identity \(F\,\sigma(F)=aX^h+bY^h\), with \(\sigma\) the nontrivial automorphism over \(K_+\). The order of \(F\) along \(Y=1\) must be zero, since that divisor is fixed by \(\sigma\). Specialization gives \(f(X)\sigma(f)(X)=aX^h+b\).

At the prime over \(3\) in \(L_+\), \(v(3)=2\) and \(v(r_+)=1\), so \(v(b/a)=2n_0+1\) is odd. Every irreducible factor of the binomial has degree divisible by \(h/\gcd(h,2n_0+1)\), by taking the valuation of the product of its roots. The binomial is squarefree. A factor fixed by \(\sigma\) would occur to even exponent in \(f\sigma(f)\), so all factors would have to occur in conjugate pairs. Their total degree would then be divisible by \(2h/\gcd(h,2n_0+1)\), which does not divide \(h\). This rules out the fixed rational-function construction, including Laurent polynomials. It leaves variable lifts and density of genuinely admissible heights unresolved.

### Exact controls

At \(t=7\), the lifts below give \(25^7-9^7=23816^2+3\cdot42940^2\). Conjugating the plus lift by \(v_+\mapsto-v_+\) preserves its \(E\)-norm and lands on the conjugate signed target. At \(t=23\), the inert prime \(47\) has valuation one in the plus half, so either orientation fails to lift.

```python
from fractions import Fraction as F

# Coordinates of beta are (a + b*u + c*v + d*u*v)/2.
def nk(s, a, b, c, d):
    return F(a*a+3*b*b-5*s*c*c-15*s*d*d, 4), F(a*d-b*c, 2)

def ne(s, a, b, c, d):
    return F(a*a-3*b*b-5*s*c*c+15*s*d*d, 4), F(a*b-5*s*c*d, 2)

def mul(z, w):
    a,b = z
    c,d = w
    return a*c-3*b*d, a*d+b*c

bp, bm = (17,-4,1,2), (15,1,8,-6)
assert (bp[0]-bp[1]-bp[2]+bp[3]) % 2 == 0
assert (bm[0]-bm[1]) % 2 == (bm[2]-bm[3]) % 2 == 0
assert nk(1,*bp) == (68,19) and nk(-1,*bm) == (272,-49)
eta_p = ne(1,*bp)
eta_m = mul(ne(-1,*bm), (F(1,2),F(1,2)))  # rotate by an Eisenstein unit
x,y = (4*z for z in mul(eta_p,eta_m))
assert (x,y) == (23816,-42940) and x*x+3*y*y == 25**7-9**7

wrong = (17,-4,-1,-2)
assert ne(1,*wrong) == eta_p and nk(1,*wrong) == (68,-19)
H23 = (5**23+3**23)//8
assert H23 % 47 == 0 and H23 % 47**2 != 0
```

Independent BigInt checked both rationally scaled relative norms, the known whole norm, the conjugate lift and the inert no-lift case. The displayed numerical coordinates allow an Eisenstein unit rotation of the transfer, which preserves the norm. The rational-function barrier is not an exclusion of algebraic or variable lifts.
