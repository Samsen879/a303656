# Independent repository proof of MF-R

All rings below are over `Q`; `i^2=-1`. Write `c_j=X-A_j` and
`z_j=a_j+i b_j`. The presentations below are derived here from the defining
quadrics; no packet proof is imported.

## Domain lemma, including repeated offsets

As a `Q[X,a_1,...,a_k]`-module, `R` is free on the `2^k` monomials
`prod_j b_j^epsilon_j` (`epsilon_j` is zero or one): each defining equation
is monic in its distinct `b_j`. Base extension `Q -> Q(i)` is faithfully flat,
so `R -> R tensor_Q Q(i)` is injective. The same monic normal form makes
`R tensor_Q Q(i)` torsion-free over `Q[X]`. With `S=Q[X] \ {0}`,
localization at `S` is therefore injective. In this generic fibre,
setting `z_j=a_j+i b_j` gives the presentation

```text
S^-1(R tensor_Q Q(i))
  = (R tensor_Q Q(i)) tensor_{Q(i)[X]} Q(i)(X)
  = Q(i)(X)[z_1^±1,...,z_k^±1],
a_j=(z_j+c_j/z_j)/2,  b_j=(z_j-c_j/z_j)/(2i).
```

Indeed `z_j(a_j-i b_j)=c_j`, and each `c_j` is invertible in `Q(i)(X)`,
so every `z_j` is invertible; the displayed inverse formulas prove the
isomorphism. This Laurent ring is a domain, and both maps into it are
injective, so `R` is a domain and
`K(i)=Q(i)(X,z_1,...,z_k)`. The argument never requires distinct `A_j`.

## Route A: invariant prime valuation

Localize `R tensor_Q Q(i)` by all `c_j`. The same formulas identify the
localized ring with

```text
B = Q(i)[X,c_1^-1,...,c_k^-1,z_1^±1,...,z_k^±1].
```

This is a Laurent polynomial ring over a localization of `Q(i)[X]`.
Conjugation fixes `X`, sends `i` to `-i`, and sends
`z_j` to `c_j/z_j`; it is an involution of `B`.

Suppose `T` differs from every `A_j`. Then `pi=X-T` is a prime element of
`B`, since no `c_j` vanishes at `X=T`. The associated discrete valuation on
`Frac(B)=K(i)` is invariant under conjugation: the involution fixes `pi`
and preserves units of the local ring `B_(pi)`. Thus for every nonzero
`F in K(i)`, including functions with arbitrary poles,

```text
v_pi(F conjugate(F)) = 2 v_pi(F),
```

an even integer (negative valuations are allowed). If `U,V in K` satisfied
the identity, `F=U+iV` would have norm `F conjugate(F)=X-T`, whose valuation
is one. This is impossible. The converse follows from the source coordinates.

## Route B: target fibre and primitive denominator

Independently fix `T` distinct from all `A_j`, set `pi=X-T`, and consider
`C=R/(pi)`. Over `Q(i)`, each `c_j` becomes the nonzero rational number
`T-A_j`. The change of variables above therefore identifies

```text
C tensor_Q Q(i) = Q(i)[z_1^±1,...,z_k^±1].
```

Consequently `C` is a domain. Its fraction field cannot contain `i`:
otherwise extending that field from `Q` to `Q(i)` would split the polynomial
`t^2+1` and create zero divisors, whereas the displayed Laurent-domain
presentation remains a domain after localization. Thus `x^2+y^2=0` over
`Frac(C)` forces `x=y=0`.

Since `C=R/(pi)` is a domain, `(pi)` is prime in the Noetherian domain `R`.
Also `pi` is nonzero in `R`. The principal ideal theorem gives
`ht(pi)<=1`, while the chain `(0) proper-subset (pi)` gives `ht(pi)>=1`;
thus `ht(pi)=1`. The local domain `R_(pi)` is Noetherian of dimension one,
and its maximal ideal is generated by `pi`. It is therefore a regular local
domain of dimension one, hence a discrete valuation ring. Its residue field is
`Frac(C)`. Put any proposed `U,V` over one common denominator in this DVR,
and remove their common power of `pi`: write `U=P/D,V=Q/D` with
`P,Q,D in R_(pi)` and at least one of `P,Q,D` a unit. The norm identity says

```text
P^2+Q^2=pi D^2.
```

Reduction modulo `pi` and anisotropy in `Frac(C)` force `P,Q` into `(pi)`.
Then the left side lies in `(pi^2)`, so `D` lies in `(pi)`. This contradicts
the primitive choice. Arbitrary rational poles are covered by the common
denominator and valuation normalization. Again `T=A_j` is realized by
`a_j,b_j`.

## Finite-chart corollary

Fix finitely many rational charts on this full product, with each target
offset `T_l` outside `{A_j}`. For each chart, clear its denominators in
`U_l^2+V_l^2-(X-T_l)`. MF-R says the resulting norm-defect numerator is a
nonzero polynomial in the domain `R`; its zero set is proper closed. The
chart's pole set is another proper closed set. Its successful decoding locus
away from poles lies in the defect zero set. A finite union of these proper
closed sets cannot contain a nonempty Zariski-open subset of the irreducible
full source product. This is a geometric statement; it gives no density claim
for integer witnesses.
