# WHY n ≡ 560 mod 2520?

## Verdict: PARTIALLY EXPLAINED

A **proved local-minimizer mechanism** explains its core signature. It does not explain why this particular refinement by 5 and 9 was singled out more strongly in the reported finite sample.

The exact 2/3/7 minimum is reached whenever

7|n and n mod24 is 8 or 22,

not only at 560 mod2520. Its exact density is 21/128. The 560 class is one of THIRTY minimizing classes modulo 2520. The full coefficient has period 504, while the equality set has reduced period 168. Neither 5|n nor the specified n mod9 lift is required for equality.

The equivalence in the brief is correct:

n=560 mod2520 iff 280|n and n/280=2 mod9.

It gives n=0 mod8, n=2 mod9, n=0 mod5, n=0 mod7. Below is what those conditions actually do.

## 1. The 2-adic and 3-adic coupling

Modulo 8, powers of 3 are 1,3 according as c is even,odd, and powers of 5 are 1,5 according as d is even,odd. For n=0 mod8:

| | d even | d odd |
|---|---|---|
|c even|m=6 mod8: impossible|m=2 mod8: passes L2|
|c odd|m=4 mod8: half of higher lifts pass|m=0 mod8: half of higher lifts pass|

For c>=2, 3^c vanishes modulo 9. The six powers of 5 modulo 9 are

1,5,7,8,4,2.

Since n=2 mod9, the corresponding residual residues are

1,6,4,3,7,0.

Thus d=1,3 mod6 have v3(m)=1 and fail. Even d are 3-adic units. At d=5 mod6 the residual is 0 mod9, and exactly a 3/4 proportion of higher 3-adic lifts have even valuation.

The positive-weight exponent cells are therefore:

A: c odd, d even, each with weight 1/2;
B: d=5 mod6, any c, with weight 3/4 for even c and 3/8 for odd c.

These account for 15 of the 36 coarse cells. Their weighted total is 7/32, not 15/36. In particular a finite quotient test modulo 72 leaves 5/12, while full 2/3 parity integration leaves 7/32. The two quantities have different meanings.

This illustrates the source of the bad coupling: the even-c states which pass the 2-adic test require d odd, and most odd-d states are then killed by v3=1. Multiplying unconditional independent 2- and 3-adic proportions would miss the alignment.

### Transient rows must not be dropped from finite representations

For c=0, the mod9 residual cycle is 0,5,3,2,6,8. For c=1 it is 7,3,1,0,4,6. These differ from the c>=2 cycle. The finite counterexample/representation task includes them. They disappear only from the limiting density or in an explicitly bounded O(log n) error. The same care applies to d=0 in moduli divisible by 5.

## 2. Why 7 is especially efficient on the survivors

Modulo 7, powers of 3 run through 1,3,2,6,4,5, while powers of 5 run through 1,5,4,6,2,3. Since 5=3^{-1} and -1=3^3 modulo 7,

7|m  iff  c+d=3 mod6.

Among the 15 positive-weight cells above, precisely four hit 7:

(c,d)=(1,2),(3,0),(5,4),(4,5) mod6.

Their pre-7 weighted mass is

(3*(1/2)+3/4)/36=1/16.

On a 7-divisible coset, the odd-valuation mass is 7/8 and the even-valuation mass is 1/8. Hence 7 removes

(7/8)*(1/16)=7/128,

leaving

7/32-7/128=21/128.

That loss is ONE QUARTER of the 2/3-admissible mass. It is a precise least-bad-7 capacity after the L2/L3 filters, rather than a CD/7 heuristic. Without those filters, n=0 mod7 has six divisibility cells out of 36 versus five for a nonzero n mod7. The much stronger conditioned effect comes from the coupling, not merely from raw divisibility frequency.

The proof that this is a GLOBAL minimum, and the exhaustive equality cases, are in Theorem K-L2 of LOCAL_MODEL.md.

## 3. Why 5 and the particular mod9 refinement are not yet explained

At any power of 5 there is no local sum-of-two-squares obstruction. Over the residue ring a square root i of -1 exists, and (m+1)^2/4 + (m-1)^2/(4i^2)=m. The denominators are units. Therefore adding a mod5 norm test cannot make one of the 2/3/7 minimizers uniquely harder.

Changing n mod9 among the compatible three lifts changes which odd d class receives the weight 3/4. The 7-hit mass remains 1/16 for n mod24=8 or 22 and 7|n. The finite certificate in LOCAL_MODEL.md checks all three possibilities. Thus the choice n=2 mod9 is not uniquely minimizing either.

This does not prove that the observed enrichment is accidental: finite-height boundaries, omitted primes, and selection conventions could distinguish equal-coefficient classes. Their actual contribution cannot be inferred without the saved full-window data and exact metric definitions.

**560, 8120, 18200, 20720, 23240 mod27720.**

## 5. Extra local rigidity, but not a Gaussian completion

On the A branch, c is odd, d is even, and m=4 mod8. Full L2 then requires m=4 mod16. Substituting powers modulo 16 gives

c+d = 3+n/4 mod4.

For n=0 mod16 this is c+d=3 mod4; for n=8 mod16 it is c+d=1 mod4. If the cell also hits 7, combining c+d=3 mod6 gives c+d=3 mod12 or 9 mod12 respectively.

These are genuine exponent constraints, but they do not force a Gaussian norm. For example, both targets below are 560 mod27720 and 8 mod16, with the SAME exponents (c,d)=(3,2):

28280-3^3-5^2 = 28228 = 2^2*7057, a norm;
915320-3^3-5^2 = 915268 = 2^2*19*12043, not a norm.

All displayed odd factors are proved prime by trial division in the reference verifier. Both residuals pass the 2,3,7,11 tests and the same local exponent rigidity. In the second, the least bad prime is 19 and a second bad prime is 12043. This is not a claim that 915320 is unrepresentable. It is a concrete obstruction to concluding that the surviving exponent subclass alone has an automatic norm identity.

## 6. First-power modulus warning

Even within 560 mod27720 and for a fixed exponent pair, full 7-parity can change:

560-3^1-5^2 = 532 = 2^2*7*19;
277760-3^1-5^2 = 277732 = 2^2*7^2*13*109 = 336^2+406^2.

These targets have the same residue modulo 27720. Thus that residue and (c,d) do not determine v7 parity. This does not contradict the fixed-period limiting density, which averages higher exponent lifts; it rules out substituting a first-power finite quotient predicate for that average.
