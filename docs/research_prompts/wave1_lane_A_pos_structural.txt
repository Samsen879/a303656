=== LANE A: prove (POS) by a structural argument ===
{{FOCUS}}
Goal. Prove Haar{x in Ẑ : f_∞(x) = 0} = 0. Together with (R1-b) and (R1-c) this gives an almost-all theorem for A303656, which as far as the repo knows is not in the literature. If you come to believe (POS) is false, the goal becomes describing the positive-measure zero set and its arithmetic cause.

Step 0 — Make the object explicit. Do this before any strategy, with full proofs (tag PROVED):
 1. Describe μ = lim μ_q as a measure on Ẑ. As (c,d) runs over a large box, 3^c + 5^d mod q equidistributes over (c, d) modulo the relevant periods, apart from pre-periodic small exponents of density 0. Show μ is the pushforward of Haar measure on a profinite exponent space E (make it precise: completions of Z adapted to the orders of 3 and 5 modulo all q) under (α, β) ↦ 3^α + 5^β, where the 3-adic coordinate of 3^α is 0 and the 5-adic coordinate of 5^β is 0. Check against a direct computation of μ_q for q = 8, 9, 7, 11, 25, 63.
 2. Write the local densities: for p ≡ 3 mod 4, β_p(y) = (1 + 1/p)·1[v_p(y) even]; for p ≡ 1 mod 4, β_p(y) = (1 − 1/p)(v_p(y) + 1); and the 2-adic factor. Verify each constant. Then
       f_q(x) = π ∫_E Π_{p^k || q} β_{p^k}(x − 3^α − 5^β) dHaar(α, β).
 3. The structural difficulty: the coordinates x_p of a Haar-random x in Ẑ are independent, but the factors at different primes are COUPLED through the shared exponents (α, β) via ord_p(3), ord_p(5). Quantify the coupling: for primes p ≠ p', how dependent are (α mod ord_p 3, β mod ord_p 5) and (α mod ord_p' 3, β mod ord_p' 5)? Relate this to gcds of orders and to Romanoff-type sums.

Strategies (pursue the assigned one in depth; you may borrow from the others or invent your own):
 (A1) Branching-process analogy. Treat primes or prime powers as generations. f_q is a positive martingale in q, like W_n = Z_n / m^n for a Galton–Watson process or a branching random walk, where L2-boundedness forces P(W = 0) = P(extinction). Find the arithmetic analogue of the inheritance / fixed-point argument. Identify exactly what replaces "independent subtrees" given the exponent coupling, and whether finite-level positivity (no extinction at any finite level) then forces f_∞ > 0 a.e. The (R1-d) countermodel shows the abstract statement is false: name the branching-structure property that countermodel lacks, and prove the arithmetic f has it.
 (A2) Zero–one law. Show {f_∞ = 0} is, up to null sets, invariant under a group action or measurable with respect to a tail σ-algebra, so its measure is 0 or 1; since ∫ f_∞ = π > 0 it is not 1. Candidates: changing finitely many p-adic coordinates x_p (study when replacing x_p can create zeros: the weight β_p(x_p − ·) vanishes on a clopen set of exponents), or maps that permute the shift set. Handle the coupling from Step 0.3 honestly.
 (A3) Restriction to a sub-measure. f_∞ >= f_∞^{(T)}, where only exponents in a sub-family T ⊂ E contribute (a coset of an open subgroup, or a set on which the relevant orders avoid a fixed set of primes). Find T for which the cross-prime coupling factorizes or is controllable, so positivity reduces to an independent-product or independent-sum statement you can prove (Kolmogorov three-series, or a second-moment argument in the exponent variable at fixed x).
 (A4) Self-similarity. Look for exact or approximate self-similarities of the shift set under c ↦ c + 1, d ↦ d + 1 (and their effect mod q) that act on f through explicit Markov-type operators, and prove positivity via a Perron–Frobenius or irreducibility argument.

Deliverables besides the RESULT CARD: Step 0 written out (PROVED); the chosen strategy with a full proof or the exact GAP; if you believe (POS) fails, a candidate positive-measure zero set with its arithmetic mechanism.
