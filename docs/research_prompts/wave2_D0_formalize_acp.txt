=== TRACK D0: formalize the ACP and audit the dichotomy logic ===
The other tracks depend on getting this right. Run it first or in parallel.
1. From c21_frozen/PROOF.md and the two C22 referee reports, extract the exact reduction from "f_q(a) = 0" to a covering statement. State precisely: the probability space after the parities are fixed; the bad event attached to each joint-nonregular prime; how regular norm primes enter (regular averaging, elimination in decreasing order of the largest order prime, conversion of a regular norm prime M into an artificial unit target); and the handling of the prime 3 and the 2-adic condition. Then write the ACP as a precise combinatorial statement on a finite exponent quotient that a program could decide for a given finite configuration.
2. Is the C21 reduction an equivalence or only a sufficient condition? Unit-avoidance (s(e) ≢ z_p mod p) is SUFFICIENT for positivity at p, but a zero needs v_p(a − s(e)) odd and < k on a full-measure set; exponent classes with s(e) ≡ a (mod p^2) can still give positivity, and for k >= 3 the p^3-level behaviour of a joint-nonregular prime need not be rigid. Decide whether the true zero condition is exactly a covering by unit-avoidance failures or something strictly stronger. A stronger condition makes (N) easier; say precisely how much easier.
3. Prove the bridge implications: (N) ⇒ LP; (Y-finite) plus a direct check of the listed primes ⇒ LP; and what exactly (Y-infinite) does and does not imply. Give a criterion that would decide (Y-finite) versus (Y-infinite).
4. Sanity tests: check that your formalization reproduces C21's conclusion on its own catalogue (no covering with <= 21 targets), and test it on toy variants (other bases, or artificial small orders) where the answer can be computed by brute force.
Deliverable: the ACP as a finite, machine-checkable statement with a reference decision procedure (code), plus proofs of the bridge implications, so the D1 and D2 sessions and later referees all work on the same object.
