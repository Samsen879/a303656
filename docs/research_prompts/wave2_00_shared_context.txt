THE MODEL (eventual-power model; use this notation).
  E = Ẑ^2 with Haar measure, e = (α, β). T_b(α) ∈ Ẑ has p-adic coordinate b^α for p ≠ b and 0 for p = b. s(e) = T_3(α) + T_5(β).
  β_{p^k}(y) = local density of u^2 + v^2 ≡ y (mod p^k). For inert p (p ≡ 3 mod 4) at precision p^k, β_{p^k}(y) = 0 iff v_p(y) is odd and < k.
  f_q(a) = π E_e[ Π_{p^k || q} β_{p^k}(a − s(e)) ].
  LP: f_q(a) > 0 for every q and every residue a.

  Facts: such a p is a base-3 Wieferich prime; the only known ones are 11 and 1006003 (no others below 9.7e14, Dorais–Klyve, OEIS A014127), and neither is base-5 Wieferich. No joint-nonregular prime is known.
  Consequence (CONDITIONAL): if at most 22 joint-nonregular primes ≡ 3 mod 4 exist, then almost all n are representable.

HOW C21/C22 WORK (so you can extend or break them).
  Regular inert primes (non-Wieferich for at least one of the bases 3, 5) never create a zero by themselves: averaging over that base's p-primary exponent component multiplies the mass by a factor in [1/p, 1 + 1/p] ("regular averaging"). The prime 3 and the 2-adic condition are absorbed by fixing the exponent parities.
  A joint-nonregular p is rigid: s(e) mod p^2 depends only on e modulo (ord_p 3, ord_p 5), so "v_p(a − s(e)) = 1" can hold on whole exponent classes. A sufficient condition for positivity at p is s(e) ≢ z_p (mod p) ("unit-avoidance target").
  Per-target bound: with parities fixed, Pr[s(e) ≡ z (mod p)] <= 1/H_p, where H_p = max(oddpart ord_p 3, oddpart ord_p 5). Exact counts give sharper bounds for 11, 71, 271.
  Induction on M = max odd prime factor of L_p = lcm(ord_p 3, ord_p 5) over the targets: with t active targets (M | L_p), the fresh M-primary exponent fibre gives each bad event probability <= 1/M, so every case with t < M is automatic. A regular norm prime equal to M is converted into a new unit target ("artificial target"); this is why regular primes cannot simply be deleted.
  Critical cases t >= M are closed by explicit union-bound budgets over a complete catalogue (odd 19-smooth h < 512; every unknown prime has weight <= 1/512). With <= 22 targets only M ∈ {11, 13, 17, 19} are critical, and the worst budget is 4238011529/4390848000 at M = t = 11. With 23 targets the case M = t = 23 appears, where t/M = 1 and the union bound fails; every larger prime M has the same t = M critical case.

THE DICHOTOMY QUESTION.
  ABSTRACT COVERING PROBLEM (ACP). Take a finite set T of ACTUAL primes p > 3, p ≡ 3 mod 4, and treat them AS IF they were joint-nonregular: their true Wieferich status is ignored, while the arithmetic of F_p (orders, discrete logs, the addition table of <3> and <5>) is real. Allow arbitrary targets, arbitrary regular-prime norm conditions and an arbitrary 2-adic condition. Can the resulting bad events cover E up to measure zero, i.e. produce a zero of f_q in this pretend model?
  (N) No configuration covers, for any finite T: then LP holds unconditionally, and almost-all becomes an unconditional theorem.
  (Y-finite) Coverings exist, but every covering uses primes from an explicit finite list: check those primes directly (they are actual primes, so this is a pow() computation); if none is joint-nonregular, LP still follows unconditionally.
  (Y-infinite) Coverings exist for infinitely many prime sets: then local arguments cannot prove LP, LP genuinely depends on the (unknown) existence of joint-nonregular primes, and the conditional theorem is essentially the final form of this route. The useful output is then a characterization of covering configurations (minimal size, order structure), which sharpens the conditional hypothesis.
  Before anything else, pin down the exact ACP from c21_frozen/PROOF.md: which events, which conditioning, how regular primes and artificial targets enter, and whether the true zero condition (v_p(a − s(e)) odd and < k on a full-measure set) is stricter than "unit-avoidance fails".
