THE MODEL (eventual-power model)
- E = Ẑ^2 with Haar measure, e = (α, β). T_b(α) ∈ Ẑ has p-adic coordinate b^α for p ≠ b and 0 for p = b. s(e) = T_3(α) + T_5(β).
- β_{p^k}(y) = local density of u^2 + v^2 ≡ y (mod p^k). For p ≡ 3 mod 4: β_{p^k}(y) = 0 iff v_p(y) is odd and < k.
- f_q(a) = π · E_e[ Π_{p^k || q} β_{p^k}(a − s(e)) ].  LP: f_q(a) > 0 for all q and a.

WHAT IS ALREADY KNOWN (candidate results from the previous wave, AI-refereed only)
- LP ⇒ almost all n are representable (via a weighted L2 transfer and a zero-set theorem {f_∞ = 0} = ∪_q {f_q = 0} a.e.).
- C21/C22: a zero f_q(a) = 0 forces at least 23 distinct primes p > 3, p ≡ 3 mod 4, p^2 | q, that are JOINT-NONREGULAR: 3^{p−1} ≡ 5^{p−1} ≡ 1 (mod p^2). No such prime is known: any one would be a base-3 Wieferich prime, the only known ones are 11 and 1006003 (none else below 9.7e14), and neither is base-5 Wieferich.
- Mechanism: regular primes never create zeros (averaging over a regular base's p-primary exponent component keeps mass within a factor [1/p, 1 + 1/p]). The prime 3 and the 2-adic condition are absorbed by fixing exponent parities. A joint-nonregular p is rigid (s(e) mod p^2 depends only on e mod (ord_p 3, ord_p 5)), so v_p(a − s(e)) = 1 can hold on whole exponent classes. Positivity at p is guaranteed if s(e) ≢ z_p (mod p).
- With parities fixed, Pr[s(e) ≡ z (mod p)] <= 1/H_p, where H_p = max(oddpart ord_p 3, oddpart ord_p 5).
- Induction is on M = the largest odd prime factor of L_p = lcm(ord_p 3, ord_p 5). With t active targets (M | L_p), the fresh M-primary fibre gives each bad event probability <= 1/M, so t < M is automatic. A regular norm prime equal to M becomes an "artificial" unit target. Critical cases t >= M are closed by union-bound budgets over a complete catalogue (odd 19-smooth h < 512). This works up to 22 targets; at 23 targets the case M = t = 23 appears, the union bound gives t/M = 1, and every larger prime M repeats this.

THE QUESTION (Abstract Covering Problem, ACP)
Take a finite set T of ACTUAL primes p > 3, p ≡ 3 mod 4, and treat them AS IF they were joint-nonregular. Ignore their real Wieferich status; keep the real arithmetic of F_p (orders, discrete logs, the addition table of <3> and <5>). Allow arbitrary targets, regular-prime norm conditions and a 2-adic condition. Can the bad events cover E up to measure zero, i.e. produce a zero of f_q in this pretend model?
- (N) Never, for any finite T: then LP holds unconditionally and almost-all becomes an unconditional theorem.
- (Y-finite) Coverings exist, but only with primes from an explicit finite list: check those primes directly with pow(); if none is joint-nonregular, LP still follows.
- (Y-infinite) Coverings exist for infinitely many prime sets: then LP cannot be proved by local arguments, and the conditional theorem above is essentially the end of this route.
