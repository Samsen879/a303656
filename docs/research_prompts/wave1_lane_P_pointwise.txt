=== LANE P: every large n — conditional theorems, a weak ML-K2, or explicit infinite families ===
{{FOCUS}}
Read first: analysis/direct_original_problem_phase_i_k2/{MISSING_LEMMA.md, THEOREM_ASSETS.md} and sources/k2/{LARGE_PRIME_DECOMPOSITION.md, PAIR_COMPLETION.md, COUNTEREXAMPLES.md}.

Tracks:
 (P1) Conditional universality. Find the weakest clearly stated hypothesis H, preferably a standard named conjecture or a precise generalization of one (GRH for Hecke L-functions of Q(i); Elliott–Halberstam-type statements; Hooley-type hypotheses; a Hardy–Littlewood / Bateman–Horn-type statement for sums of two squares along exponential sequences; effective abc- or Schanuel-type inputs), and PROVE: H ⇒ R(n) >= 1 for all n >= N0, with N0 effective if possible. Be honest about whether H is standard or merely a restatement; a hypothesis equivalent to the conjecture is worthless. The real difficulty: a fixed n has only Θ((log n)^2) residuals, so any distributional input must be pointwise in n, not averaged.
 (P2) Weak ML-K2. With the notation of (R1-a), prove S(n) <= G(n) − 1 for all large n (weaker than ML-K2 and still sufficient). Facts to exploit or refute: a failing residual in G has >= 2 distinct odd-valuation primes ≡ 3 mod 4 (parity from the 2-adic filter), so it is >= pq; a large prime p obstructs only residuals divisible by p, and those lie on the line 3^c + 5^d ≡ n mod p in <3> × <5>. Bound #{(c,d) <= (C, D) : 3^c + 5^d ≡ n mod p} uniformly in p and n (an S-unit / exponential-sum counting problem), then try to show the large primes cannot jointly cover G. The repo's K2-N3 integer-distance barrier (sources/k2/COUNTEREXAMPLES.md) rules out per-prime absolute-error templates; do not use one.
 (P3) Explicit infinite families beyond positive density. Prove R(n) >= 1 for every n in an explicitly described infinite set that is NOT a trivial consequence of the definition (for example a full residue class intersected with long intervals, or all n in a neighbourhood of 5^D of a given shape), and explain what makes the family tractable.
