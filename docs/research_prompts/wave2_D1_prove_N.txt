=== TRACK D1: prove (N) — no abstract covering exists, so LP holds unconditionally ===
{{FOCUS}}
Goal. Prove that for every finite set T of primes p > 3, p ≡ 3 mod 4 treated as joint-nonregular, for every choice of targets, regular-prime norm conditions and 2-adic condition, the good set has positive Haar measure. Equivalently: extend C21/C22 from "<= 22 targets" to "any number of targets". If that is out of reach, prove (Y-finite): coverings can only use primes from an explicit finite list, then check that list.
The obstruction. In C21's induction, the critical cases t >= M (t active targets with M | L_p on a fresh M-primary fibre) are closed by a union bound, which fails at t = M. A proof has to replace the union bound with something that uses the arithmetic of the targets.
Strategies:
 (D1a) Local lemma. A bad event at p depends only on the exponent prime-power coordinates (α_ℓ, β_ℓ) with ℓ | L_p, once the 2-parts are fixed. Build this dependency structure and apply the Lovász Local Lemma (lopsided version, or Shearer's bound) in the critical fibres. Decide whether, with per-event probabilities <= 1/H_p and the actual overlap pattern of the L_p, the LLL certifies positivity uniformly in M and t, possibly after C21's catalogue has handled the small orders.
 (D1b) Exact tiling via Fourier analysis. In a critical fibre the bad events must cover with total probability close to 1, so they must nearly tile. Each bad set is an "exponential curve" {(α, β) : 3^α + 5^β ≡ z (mod p)} on (Z/ord_p 3) × (Z/ord_p 5). Use additive characters of F_p and the multiplicative structure of <3> and <5> (Gauss sums; Weil-type bounds on point counts when the orders are large; exact structure when they are small) to show that the required tiling identities cannot hold. Make precise which Fourier coefficients a covering would force to vanish, and why they cannot.
 (D1c) Counting with gcd bounds. A prime with odd orders (h3, h5) divides gcd(3^{2·h3} − 1, 5^{2·h5} − 1). Find and quote exactly the best available bounds on such gcds (Bugeaud–Corvaja–Zannier for equal exponents, and whatever exists for unequal exponents) and use them to bound the total bad mass that primes of each order class can place on a fresh fibre. Combine this with the induction to rule out coverings for M >= M0, and settle M < M0 by an exhaustive finite computation with a verifier.
Whatever you prove must be uniform in M and in |T|. If you only get a partial extension (for example up to 30 targets, or all M >= M0), state it exactly.
